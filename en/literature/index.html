Literature

The bibliography and lecture reading lists come from the notes.

Books

The per-lecture References sections cite the works below by author and short title, with printed pages. Several use conventions different from this course’s (the slot in which the character inner product is antilinear, the order of composition, D2nD_{2n}D2n​ for our DnD_nDn​, French Young diagrams); the lectures translate at the point of use (see the Notation Bible).

  • D. S. Dummit and R. M. Foote, Abstract Algebra, 3rd ed. Wiley, 2004.

  • J. F. Humphreys, A Course in Group Theory. Oxford University Press, 1996.

  • I. M. Isaacs, Finite Group Theory. AMS (Graduate Studies in Mathematics 92), 2008.

  • J. J. Rotman, An Introduction to the Theory of Groups, 4th ed. Springer (GTM 148), 1995.

  • J.-P. Serre, Linear Representations of Finite Groups. Springer (GTM 42), 1977.

  • G. James and M. Liebeck, Representations and Characters of Groups, 2nd ed. Cambridge University Press, 2001.

  • P. Etingof, O. Golberg, S. Hensel, T. Liu, A. Schwendner, D. Vaintrob and E. Yudovina, Introduction to Representation Theory. AMS (Student Mathematical Library 59), 2011; arXiv:0901.0827.

  • T. Ceccherini-Silberstein, F. Scarabotti and F. Tolli, Representation Theory of the Symmetric Groups: The Okounkov–Vershik Approach, Character Formulas, and Partition Algebras. Cambridge University Press, 2010.

  • A. M. Vershik and A. Yu. Okounkov, A new approach to the representation theory of the symmetric groups. II. J. Math. Sci. 131 (2005); arXiv:math/0503040.

  • M. Lorenz, A Tour of Representation Theory. AMS (Graduate Studies in Mathematics 193), 2018.

  • I. G. Macdonald, Symmetric Functions and Hall Polynomials, 2nd ed. Oxford University Press, 1995.

  • B. E. Sagan, The Symmetric Group, 2nd ed. Springer (GTM 203), 2001.

  • W. Fulton and J. Harris, Representation Theory: A First Course. Springer (GTM 129), 1991.

  • R. Goodman and N. R. Wallach, Symmetry, Representations, and Invariants. Springer (GTM 255), 2009.

Use the reading list for the result you are studying.

Part I — Groups and Characters

Lecture 1 · Groups and symmetries

Dummit and Foote, Abstract Algebra, §1.2, pp. 23–26, use D2nD_{2n}D2n​ for the group denoted here by Dn\Dih{n}Dn​.

Lecture 2 · Actions, cosets and orbit–stabiliser

Dummit and Foote, Abstract Algebra, actions in §1.7, pp. 41–43, orbit–stabiliser in §4.1, Proposition 2, pp. 114–115, and Cayley’s theorem in §4.2, Corollary 4, p. 120. Rotman, An Introduction to the Theory of Groups, Lagrange’s theorem, Theorem 2.11, p. 26, and orbit–stabiliser, Theorem 3.19, p. 57.

Lecture 3 · Conjugation, the class equation and counting orbits

Dummit and Foote, Abstract Algebra, the class equation in §4.3, Proposition 6 and Theorem 7, pp. 123–124. Rotman, An Introduction to the Theory of Groups, orbit counting, Theorem 3.22, pp. 58–59. Humphreys, A Course in Group Theory, Ch. 10, pp. 89–94.

Lecture 4 · Normal subgroups, quotients and the isomorphism theorems

Dummit and Foote, Abstract Algebra, §3.1 on quotients, §3.2 on index two and §3.3 on the isomorphism theorems. Rotman, An Introduction to the Theory of Groups, Theorem 2.28, p. 38, on subgroup correspondence.

Lecture 5 · Direct and semidirect products

Dummit and Foote, Abstract Algebra, §5.4, Theorem 9, p. 171, and §5.5, Theorem 12, p. 180. Rotman, An Introduction to the Theory of Groups, pp. 167–170, on semidirect products.

Lecture 6 · The symmetric group: cycles, classes and sign

Dummit and Foote, Abstract Algebra, §3.5 on sign and An\AG{n}An​ and §4.3 on the classes of Sn\SG{n}Sn​. Sagan, The Symmetric Group, Proposition 1.1.1 and (1.2), p. 3, on zμz_\muzμ​ and class sizes.

Lecture 7 · Cauchy, ppp-groups and Sylow existence

Dummit and Foote, Abstract Algebra, the class equation in §4.3, Proposition 6 and Theorem 7, pp. 123–124, used here for ppp-groups and in the existence proof.

Lecture 8 · Sylow conjugacy and counting

Dummit and Foote, Abstract Algebra, orbit–stabiliser in §4.1, Proposition 2, pp. 114–115, and semidirect products in §5.5, Theorem 12, p. 180.

Lecture 9 · Simple groups and Jordan–Hölder

Dummit and Foote, Abstract Algebra, §3.5 on sign and An\AG{n}An​ and §4.3 on conjugacy classes.

Lecture 10 · Solvable and nilpotent groups

Dummit and Foote, Abstract Algebra, §3.1 on quotients and §3.3 on the isomorphism theorems underlying abelianisation. Rotman, An Introduction to the Theory of Groups, pp. 167–170, on semidirect products, used here for the pairing stabiliser.

Lecture 11 · Representations and Maschke’s theorem

Serre, Linear Representations of Finite Groups, Theorem 8, p. 21, for averaging as the projection onto the trivial summand. Etingof et al., Introduction to Representation Theory, §5.1, on invariant Hermitian inner products.

Lecture 12 · Schur’s lemma and multiplicities

Serre, Linear Representations of Finite Groups, Theorem 8, p. 21, on the canonical decomposition by isomorphism type. Dummit and Foote, Abstract Algebra, §1.2, pp. 23–26, for the dihedral relations used in the square-plane example.

Lecture 13 · Characters and orthonormality

Serre, Linear Representations of Finite Groups, Theorem 8, p. 21, for the averaging projection onto the invariant subspace. Etingof et al., Introduction to Representation Theory, §5.3, on expressing character values as sums of roots of unity.

Lecture 14 · Character tables

Serre, Linear Representations of Finite Groups, Proposition 5 and its corollaries, p. 18, on the regular representation, and Theorems 6–7 and Proposition 7, pp. 19–20, on completeness and column orthogonality. Dummit and Foote, Abstract Algebra, §3.3, for the quotient factorisation used in lifting.

Part II — Representations and the Symmetric Group

Lecture 15 · The group algebra

Serre, Linear Representations of Finite Groups, §6.1, p. 47, on the group algebra, Proposition 10, p. 48, on the matrix blocks, and §6.3, p. 50, on class sums and the centre. The projection formula is Theorem 8, p. 21. Serre takes the character inner product antilinear in the second slot, while ours is antilinear in the first.

Lecture 16 · Commutants and double centralisers

Serre, Linear Representations of Finite Groups, Theorem 8, p. 21, on the canonical decomposition and its projections. Ceccherini-Silberstein, Scarabotti and Tolli, Representation Theory of the Symmetric Groups, Theorem 1.2.14 and Corollary 1.2.17, pp. 16–17, on the commutant. Etingof et al., Introduction to Representation Theory, Theorem 5.18.1, p. 120, on the double centraliser.

Lecture 17 · Restriction and induction

Serre, Linear Representations of Finite Groups, §3.3, pp. 28–31, on induction and the induced character, and Proposition 18, p. 54, on the balanced construction. Ceccherini-Silberstein, Scarabotti and Tolli, Representation Theory of the Symmetric Groups, Theorem 2.1.10, p. 64, on the centraliser algebra and the Gelfand criterion, and Definition 1.6.1 and Theorem 1.6.7, pp. 51–53, on induction and its character.

Lecture 18 · Frobenius reciprocity and Mackey’s formula

Serre, Linear Representations of Finite Groups, the reciprocity theorem, Theorem 13, p. 56, the restriction formula, Proposition 22, p. 58, and Mackey’s criterion, Proposition 23, p. 59. Ceccherini-Silberstein, Scarabotti and Tolli, Representation Theory of the Symmetric Groups, Theorem 1.6.11, p. 55, on reciprocity and Theorem 1.6.14, p. 57, on Mackey’s formula. Their subgroup names HHH and KKK are interchanged relative to ours.

Lecture 19 · Algebraic integers and the degrees of irreducibles

Serre, Linear Representations of Finite Groups, §6.5, pp. 52–53, on algebraic integers and the degrees. James and Liebeck, Representations and Characters of Groups, Chapter 22, pp. 244–250, on central characters and integrality. Etingof et al., Introduction to Representation Theory, §5.1, on algebraic numbers and algebraic integers.

Lecture 20 · Burnside’s paqbp^aq^bpaqb theorem
