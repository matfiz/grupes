Lecture 25

Content vectors and standard tableaux

We turn the restrictions on weights into three conditions on a tuple. A filling of a diagram gives the same conditions. We prove that the tuple determines the filling and that every tuple satisfying the conditions comes from one.

Where we are

For a weight, adjacent entries are unequal. Swaps with difference other than 111 or −1-1−1 stay inside the same representation (Theorem 24.3 and Lemma 24.9). Consecutive triples a,a±1,aa,a\pm1,aa,a±1,a are excluded by the braid relation (Lemma 24.8). Truncation removes the last weight entry and passes to the smaller group (Lemma 24.10).

25.1 Reading the contents of boxes

A partition records the lengths of rows, with the longest at the top. We use the positions of its boxes to attach integers to a filling of the rows.

The shape is λ\lambdaλ. The content of box b=(i,j)b=(i,j)b=(i,j) is c(b)=j−i\cont(b)=j-ic(b)=j−i. Boxes of a fixed content form a diagonal.

A standard Young tableau fills the diagram with 1,…,n1,\dots,n1,…,n, each once, increasing along the rows and down the columns. Write SYT⁡(λ)\SYT(\lambda)SYT(λ) for the set of such tableaux of shape λ\lambdaλ and SYT⁡(n)\SYT(n)SYT(n) for their union over the partitions of nnn. 25.3 Reconstructing the tableau

For a standard tableau, the entry of a box is greater than the entry of any distinct box whose row and column numbers are no larger. One can go along that box’s row and then down the column, increasing at every step. In particular, the entries of one diagonal increase as the diagonal goes down. This gives an order for placing entries with equal contents.

To reconstruct a tableau one box at a time, we also need to know which boxes we can add.

Definition 25.7 (Addable box). An addable box of a diagram is a box outside the diagram whose addition gives a Young diagram.

For a shape λ\lambdaλ the addable positions have the form (i,λi+1)(i,\lambda_i+1)(i,λi​+1) for i≤ℓ(λ)+1i\le\len(\lambda)+1i≤ℓ(λ)+1, with the convention λℓ(λ)+1=0\lambda_{\len(\lambda)+1}=0λℓ(λ)+1​=0. The content λi+1−i\lambda_i+1-iλi​+1−i strictly decreases with iii, so the addable boxes have distinct contents.

We compare a weight calculation with the box calculation at size three. The three irreducibles are 1\triv1, EEE and sgn⁡\sgnsgn by the character table in Example 13.12. Summary

  • A content vector starts at zero, has an earlier neighbour of content differing by one at each later entry, and has both neighbours between copies of any repeated entry (Definition 25.3).
  • Every weight is a content vector. Truncation handles the prefix and admissible swaps test the final entry (Theorem 25.6).
  • A content vector determines one standard tableau, reconstructed by adding a box of the required content at each step (Proposition 25.9). Theorem 26.7 will use the comparison to label irreducibles.
