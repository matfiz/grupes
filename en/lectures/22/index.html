Part II — Representations and the Symmetric Group

Branching, Gelfand–Tsetlin bases and Jucys–Murphy elements

What this lecture does

We restrict an irreducible of Sn\SG{n}Sn​ through the smaller symmetric groups until only lines remain. The choices made along the way identify the lines. The Jucys–Murphy elements give each line a tuple of eigenvalues that we can compute from characters.

Where we are

Restriction keeps the subgroup operators (Definition 17.1). The Gelfand criterion (Theorem 17.6) tests all irreducible restrictions at once: they are multiplicity-free exactly when the centraliser algebra is commutative.

Roadmap for the symmetric group

The Okounkov–Vershik approach labels the irreducibles counted by cycle types (Theorems 6.4 and 14.4). Corollary 23.5 shows that the weights distinguish path lines. The Coxeter generator si=(i  i+1)s_i=(i\,\,i{+}1)si​=(ii+1) of Proposition 6.8 is an adjacent transposition. Plain sss denotes the reflection of Dn\Dih{n}Dn​ in Example 5.7. §24.1 uses the generators to constrain the weights. Theorem 25.6 and Proposition 25.9 read the resulting content vectors as standard tableaux, and Theorem 26.7 labels irreducibles by their shapes. Theorem 27.4 gives the matrices of the adjacent transpositions in Young’s orthogonal form. The characteristic and Schur functions encode character values (Definitions 28.10 and 29.4 and Corollary 30.9). Theorem 31.11 relates them to general-linear-group characters.

22.1 Multiplicity-free branching

We embed Sk\SG{k}Sk​ in Sn\SG{n}Sn​ by fixing each point from k+1k+1k+1 to nnn. Then the subgroups and their group algebras form nested chains. At the first nontrivial restriction of a plane, we can see the splitting directly from a character.

Example 22.1 (The eigenspaces of the transposition on EEE). By Example 17.2, the restriction of EEE to S2\SG{2}S2​ is the sum of the trivial and sign lines. The lines are the 111 and −1-1−1 eigenspaces of (1 2)(1\,2)(12).

To use the Gelfand criterion for Sn−1\SG{n-1}Sn−1​, we need the conjugation orbits by the subgroup. Call these the Sn−1\SG{n-1}Sn−1​-classes of Sn\SG{n}Sn​. Their sums form the basis of the centraliser algebra by Proposition 17.5. Conjugating by a permutation that fixes nnn preserves the cycle containing nnn as a marked cycle. For instance, (1 2)(1\,2)(12) and (1 4)(1\,4)(14) have the same cycle type in S4\SG{4}S4​, but only the second has a marked cycle of length two.

Lemma 22.2 (The Sn−1\SG{n-1}Sn−1​-classes). For n≥2n\ge2n≥2, two permutations of Sn\SG{n}Sn​ are conjugate by an element of Sn−1\SG{n-1}Sn−1​ if and only if their cycle types agree and their cycles through nnn have the same length. A fixed point counts as a cycle of length one.

Proof.

  1. Read the cycles. Conjugation relabels every cycle (Theorem 6.4). The conjugator fixes nnn, so the cycle through nnn goes to the cycle through nnn.

  2. Build the relabelling. Write the marked cycle first with nnn at its start, and align all remaining cycles by length, including the fixed points: σ=(n x2 … xm)(y1 … yℓ)⋯ ,σ′=(n x2′ … xm′)(y1′ … yℓ′)⋯ .\begin{aligned} \sigma&=(n\,x_2\,\dots\,x_m)(y_1\,\dots\,y_\ell)\cdots,\\ \sigma'&=(n\,x'_2\,\dots\,x'_m)(y'_1\,\dots\,y'_\ell)\cdots. \end{aligned}σσ′​=(nx2​…xm​)(y1​…yℓ​)⋯,=(nx2′​…xm′​)(y1′​…yℓ′​)⋯.​ Send each entry to the one immediately below it. Theorem 22.4 (Multiplicity-free branching). Let n≥2n\ge2n≥2. Linear inversion fixes every element of the centraliser algebra C[Sn]Sn−1\grpalg{\SG{n}}^{\SG{n-1}}C[Sn​]Sn−1​. This algebra is commutative. Consequently Res⁡Sn−1SnU\Res{\SG{n}}{\SG{n-1}}UResSn−1​Sn​​U is multiplicity-free for every U∈Irr⁡(Sn)U\in\Irr(\SG{n})U∈Irr(Sn​).

Proof.

  1. Fix the class sums. Inversion fixes every basis class sum, since it permutes the elements of that class.

  2. Reverse products. On permutations inversion reverses the order of a product. By linearity in both factors it does the same in the group algebra. For A,BA,BA,B in the centraliser, ABABAB belongs to it too, so AB=ι(AB)=ι(B)ι(A)=BA.\begin{aligned} AB&=\iota(AB)\\ &=\iota(B)\iota(A)\\ &=BA. \end{aligned}AB​=ι(AB)=ι(B)ι(A)=BA.​

  3. Apply the criterion. Commutativity of the centraliser gives multiplicity-free restriction by the Gelfand criterion.

 

For S2≤S3\SG{2}\le\SG{3}S2​≤S3​ this argument applies to every row: the two line representations restrict to the lines with the same names and the plane restricts to two distinct lines. The two class sums (1 3)+(2 3)(1\,3)+(2\,3)(13)+(23) and (1 2 3)+(1 3 2)(1\,2\,3)+(1\,3\,2)(123)+(132) commute, even though individual permutations in their supports need not commute.

22.2 Paths and the Gelfand–Tsetlin algebra

In a multiplicity-free restriction there is one isotypic component for each occurring type, and that component is already irreducible by Proposition 16.5. We can therefore pick a component and restrict it again. A graph records the choices without choosing coordinates.

Definition 22.5 (Branching graph and paths). At level kkk the vertices of the branching graph are the irreducibles U∈Irr⁡(Sk)U\in\Irr(\SG{k})U∈Irr(Sk​). An edge U→U′U\to U'U→U′ from level k−1k-1k−1 to level kkk means UUU occurs in Res⁡Sk−1SkU′\Res{\SG{k}}{\SG{k-1}}U'ResSk−1​Sk​​U′. A path to V∈Irr⁡(Sn)V\in\Irr(\SG{n})V∈Irr(Sn​) is a chain γ=(U1→⋯→Un)\gamma=(U_1\to\dots\to U_n)γ=(U1​→⋯→Un​) with Un=VU_n=VUn​=V. A path of length nnn has nnn vertices and n−1n-1n−1 arrows.

The group S1\SG{1}S1​ is trivial, so each path starts at 1\triv1. In Figure 22.1, the two paths to EEE use the two summands of its restriction. They meet at EEE but pass through different vertices at level two.

For a path to VVV, set Yn=VY_n=VYn​=V. At step kkk take Yk−1Y_{k-1}Yk−1​ to be the Uk−1U_{k-1}Uk−1​-isotypic component in Res⁡Sk−1SkYk\Res{\SG{k}}{\SG{k-1}}Y_kResSk−1​Sk​​Yk​. Each YkY_kYk​ depends on the tail (Uk,…,Un)(U_k,\dots,U_n)(Uk​,…,Un​). The final subspace is denoted Vγ=Y1V_\gamma=Y_1Vγ​=Y1​.

To choose a path algebraically, we want to project at each step. The central idempotent for the chosen irreducible acts as identity on that type and as zero on the others (Proposition 15.12). We put all the centres that supply these operators into one algebra.

Definition 22.6 (Gelfand–Tsetlin algebra). The Gelfand–Tsetlin algebra is GZ⁡(n)=⟨Z(C[S1]),…,Z(C[Sn])⟩.\GZ{n}=\bigl\langle \Zent{\grpalg{\SG{1}}},\dots, \Zent{\grpalg{\SG{n}}}\bigr\rangle.GZ(n)=⟨Z(C[S1​]),…,Z(C[Sn​])⟩. For example the central transposition (1 2)(1\,2)(12) at level two lies in GZ⁡(3)\GZ{3}GZ(3) and distinguishes the two paths to EEE. The transposition sum at level three acts by one scalar on the whole plane.

Theorem 22.7 (Gelfand–Tsetlin bases). Let V∈Irr⁡(Sn)V\in\Irr(\SG{n})V∈Irr(Sn​).

  1. Along a path γ\gammaγ to VVV, each YkY_kYk​ is isomorphic to UkU_kUk​ and VγV_\gammaVγ​ is a line. These lines form a direct sum equal to VVV. In particular dim⁡V\dim VdimV is the number of paths to VVV.

  2. The algebra GZ⁡(n)\GZ{n}GZ(n) is commutative and acts diagonally on the path lines. For z∈Z(C[Sk])z\in\Zent{\grpalg{\SG{k}}}z∈Z(C[Sk​]), its scalar on VγV_\gammaVγ​ is ωUk(z)\omega_{U_k}(z)ωUk​​(z).

  3. The product Pγ=eU1(1)⋯eUn(n)P_\gamma=e_{U_1}^{(1)}\cdots e_{U_n}^{(n)}Pγ​=eU1​(1)​⋯eUn​(n)​ lies in GZ⁡(n)\GZ{n}GZ(n), since each factor is the central idempotent for the representation in its subscript at the indicated level.   4. In the matrix blocks of C[Sn]\grpalg{\SG{n}}C[Sn​], GZ⁡(n)\GZ{n}GZ(n) is exactly the algebra of operators diagonal on the path lines. It is maximal commutative and its dimension is the number of paths of length nnn.

  1. Split along the chain. Start at Yn=VY_n=VYn​=V. If Yk≅UkY_k\cong U_kYk​≅Uk​, the edge and multiplicity-free restriction imply that Yk−1Y_{k-1}Yk−1​ is one copy of Uk−1U_{k-1}Uk−1​. Thus Y1Y_1Y1​ is a line because S1\SG{1}S1​ has only its trivial line. At each stage the isotypic components give a direct sum, one summand for every incoming edge. Splitting every summand in turn gives the claimed direct sum.

  2. Act by scalars. For j≤kj\le kj≤k, an element central at level kkk commutes with the whole algebra at level jjj. These pairwise commuting centres generate a commutative algebra. A central element at level kkk acts on Yk≅UkY_k\cong U_kYk​≅Uk​ by its central character ωUk\omega_{U_k}ωUk​​ (Definition 19.8), and Vγ⊆YkV_\gamma\subseteq Y_kVγ​⊆Yk​. Hence every generator acts diagonally on the path lines.

  3. Project onto one path line. For another path γ′=(U1′→⋯→Un′)\gamma'=(U'_1\to\dots\to U'_n)γ′=(U1′​→⋯→Un′​), apply the central idempotent at each level to its line using the scalar action in (ii). It acts by Pγ∣Vγ′={11if every Uk=Uk′,0if some Uk≠Uk′.P_\gamma|_{V_{\gamma'}}= \begin{cases} \Id & \text{if every }U_k=U'_k,\\ 0 & \text{if some }U_k\ne U'_k. \end{cases}Pγ​∣Vγ′​​={110​if every Uk​=Uk′​,if some Uk​=Uk′​.​

  4. Find all diagonal operators. The block isomorphism of Theorem 15.9 identifies the group algebra with ⨁VEnd⁡(V)\bigoplus_V\End(V)⨁V​End(V). The scalar action in (ii) puts GZ⁡(n)\GZ{n}GZ(n) inside the algebra of diagonal operators on the path lines. Conversely, every diagonal operator is a linear combination of the PγP_\gammaPγ​ from (iii), so it lies in GZ⁡(n)\GZ{n}GZ(n). These projections are linearly independent, one per path. Summary

  • Mark the cycle through nnn to recognise Sn−1\SG{n-1}Sn−1​-classes. Inversion fixes their sums and reverses products, giving multiplicity-free restriction (Lemma 22.2 and Theorem 22.4).
  • Paths give lines. The Gelfand–Tsetlin algebra acts diagonally on them, with projections PγP_\gammaPγ​ formed from central idempotents (Theorem 22.7).
  • The operators Xk=τk−τk−1\JM{k}=\tau_k-\tau_{k-1}Xk​=τk​−τk−1​ give the weight of a path line. Their eigenvalues can be read from successive central characters (Proposition 22.11).
