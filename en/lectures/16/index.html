Commutants and double centralisers

What this lecture does

When an irreducible occurs several times, an operator can mix its copies and still commute with the group action. We describe all such operators. We then find which operators commute with all of them.

16.1 Operators that commute with the action

An intertwiner from VVV to itself commutes with every group operator. Since commutation is a linear condition on the operators it commutes with, we can impose it on the span of the group operators.

Definition 16.1 (Commutant). For a subalgebra AAA of End⁡(V)\End(V)End(V) its commutant in End⁡(V)\End(V)End(V) is A′={T∈End⁡(V):Ta=aT for every a∈A}.A'=\{T\in\End(V):Ta=aT\text{ for every }a\in A\}.A′={T∈End(V):Ta=aT for every a∈A}. Write A′′=(A′)′A''=(A')'A′′=(A′)′ for the double centraliser.

The commutant is a subalgebra because sums and products of operators commuting with AAA still commute with AAA, and the identity commutes with AAA. For the scalar algebra C11\C\IdC11, the commutant is all of End⁡(V)\End(V)End(V). For the image A=ρV(C[G])A=\rho_V(\grpalg{G})A=ρV​(C[G]) it is End⁡G(V)\End_G(V)EndG​(V) by linearity, since AAA is spanned by the group operators.

The action on each copy is the same, so the map intertwines exactly when all four blocks intertwine. Schur’s lemma (Theorem 12.2) makes each such block scalar. 16.2 Evaluation and isotypic components

In Example 16.2, the two inclusions E→E⊕EE\to E\oplus EE→E⊕E form a basis of MEM_EME​ by Schur’s lemma and the multiplicity count. For any VVV we use evaluation u⊗f↦f(u)u\otimes f\mapsto f(u)u⊗f↦f(u) to identify VVV with a sum of tensor spaces without choosing copies.

  2. Surjectivity and dimension. Split VVV into irreducibles using Maschke’s theorem (Theorem 11.11). 16.3 The double centraliser

Every element of AAA commutes with every element of A′A'A′, so A⊆A′′A\subseteq A''A⊆A′′. For a group-algebra image the commutant contains a projection onto each component and all operators on its multiplicity space. These operators suffice to force the reverse inclusion. We first study one tensor space.

Lemma 16.9 (Commutation on one tensor space). Let UUU and MMM be finite-dimensional vector spaces with M≠0M\ne0M=0. On U⊗MU\otimes MU⊗M, the commutant of the operators 11U⊗B\Id_U\otimes B11U​⊗B, B∈End⁡(M)B\in\End(M)B∈End(M), consists of all operators X⊗11MX\otimes\Id_MX⊗11M​ with X∈End⁡(U)X\in\End(U)X∈End(U).

  1. Write the blocks. Choose a basis (fj)(f_j)(fj​) of MMM. Summary

  • Evaluation identifies each isotypic component with U⊗MUU\otimes M_UU⊗MU​, and eUe_UeU​ projects onto it (Proposition 16.5).
  • The commutant acts by composition on MUM_UMU​. Its block sizes are the multiplicities mUm_UmU​ (Theorem 16.8).
  • The commutant and the group-algebra image act on opposite factors. Taking the commutant again recovers precisely the image, and the nonzero MUM_UMU​ are distinct irreducible modules for the commutant (Theorem 16.10).
