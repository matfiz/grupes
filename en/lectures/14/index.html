Where we are

Irreducible characters are orthonormal and their inner products with a character give multiplicities (Corollary 13.9). There are at most as many irreducibles as classes. For an element ggg, the class size is the index of CG(g)\Cent{G}{g}CG​(g) in GGG (Theorem 3.3). All representations here are complex and finite-dimensional, and GGG is finite.

14.2 Completeness and columns

To show the rows fill the class-function space, we test a function orthogonal to every row. Its values supply the coefficients of a sum of action operators.

A sum of operators for the two three-cycles.

This calculation is Serre’s proof in miniature. Use the values of a class function as the coefficients of a sum of action operators. The resulting operator commutes with the action, so Schur’s lemma makes it scalar on each irreducible. On the regular representation, applying the operator to eee recovers the class function from the coefficients.

14.3 Lifting from quotients and twisting by a line

A representation of a smaller group can give an action of a larger group. For example, S4\SG{4}S4​ permutes the three pairings of four letters. The resulting onto map π ⁣:S4→S3\pi\colon\SG{4}\to\SG{3}π:S4​→S3​ has kernel V4\KlV4​, the Klein four-group (§5.4). Here VVV denotes a representation, and V4\KlV4​ denotes this subgroup. The map gives S4\SG{4}S4​ an action on the plane EEE of Example 11.5. We check when this construction preserves irreducibility and how to recognise the actions that arise from it.

Theorem 14.7 (Lifting). Let N⊴GN\nsub GN⊴G and q ⁣:G→G/Nq\colon G\to G/Nq:G→G/N be the quotient map.

  1. If ρG/N\rho_{G/N}ρG/N​ is a representation of G/NG/NG/N on UUU, then its lift is the action ρ=ρG/N∘q\rho=\rho_{G/N}\circ qρ=ρG/N​∘q on UUU. It has N≤ker⁡ρN\le\ker\rhoN≤kerρ and character value χρ(g)=χρG/N(gN)\chi_\rho(g)=\chi_{\rho_{G/N}}(gN)χρ​(g)=χρG/N​​(gN). Each action with NNN in its kernel arises from exactly one action of G/NG/NG/N.

  2. A lift is irreducible exactly when the quotient action is, and two lifts are isomorphic exactly when their quotient actions are. Thus lifting bijects the irreducibles of G/NG/NG/N with the irreducibles of GGG having NNN in their kernel, up to isomorphism.

  3. The linear characters of GGG are exactly the lifts of the linear characters of G/G′G/G'G/G′.

Proof. We apply factorisation to get the actions, compare their operators, then specialise to one-dimensional actions.

  1. Actions and traces. Composition gives a homomorphism and sends elements of NNN to the identity operator. Taking traces gives the character formula. Conversely, if N≤ker⁡ρN\le\ker\rhoN≤kerρ, Theorem 4.6 gives a unique factorisation through qqq.

  2. Subspaces and maps. Since qqq is onto, the operators of ρG/N\rho_{G/N}ρG/N​ and its lift form the same set on UUU. Thus the invariant subspaces are the same. For two actions, the equations for an intertwiner of the lifts range over all cosets. They are exactly the equations for an intertwiner of the quotient actions. Invertibility is unchanged, which proves the isomorphism claim.

  3. Linear characters. A linear character takes values in the abelian group C×\C^\timesC×. The abelianisation property of Proposition 10.3 gives its unique factorisation through G/G′G/G'G/G′. Lifting from that quotient preserves the dimension of the line.

 

Thus the lift of EEE through π\piπ is irreducible: the action of S4\SG{4}S4​ has exactly the same operators on the plane EEE as the action of S3\SG{3}S3​. The lift gives a row of degree two.

We can also produce an action on a given space by multiplying each action operator by a scalar. A one-dimensional representation supplies scalars that multiply in the correct order.

Scalars commute with all operators, so the product of the operators for ggg and hhh is ρψ(g)ρψ(h)=ψ(g)ψ(h)ρ(g)ρ(h)=ψ(gh)ρ(gh).\rho_\psi(g)\rho_\psi(h)=\psi(g)\psi(h)\rho(g)\rho(h) =\psi(gh)\rho(gh).ρψ​(g)ρψ​(h)=ψ(g)ψ(h)ρ(g)ρ(h)=ψ(gh)ρ(gh). Summary

  • In the regular permutation representation, every irreducible occurs as many times as its degree. The squares of the degrees therefore sum to ∣G∣\abs G∣G∣ (Proposition 14.3).
  • The irreducible characters form a basis of the class functions. Columns for distinct classes are orthogonal, and the squared length of a column is the corresponding centraliser order (Theorem 14.4 and Corollary 14.5).
  • A quotient action lifts with the same invariant subspaces. A scalar twist multiplies each character value by the corresponding linear character value. These operations give rows of the four-letter table (Theorem 14.7, Proposition 14.8 and §14.4).
