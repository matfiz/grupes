Lemma 31.6 (Polarisation). The invariants for the place action on End⁡(V)⊗n\End(V)^{\otimes n}End(V)⊗n are spanned by a⊗na^{\otimes n}a⊗n for a∈End⁡(V)a\in\End(V)a∈End(V).

Proof. We average arbitrary tensors, identify the averages as coefficients, and show that these coefficients lie in the required span.

  1. Averaging. Each a⊗na^{\otimes n}a⊗n is fixed. The average over Sn\SG{n}Sn​ projects onto the fixed space (Proposition 11.8), so the averages of simple tensors span it.

  2. Extracting a coefficient. Take a1,…,an∈End⁡(V)a_1,\dots,a_n\in\End(V)a1​,…,an​∈End(V) and independent scalar variables t1,…,tnt_1,\dots,t_nt1​,…,tn​. Multilinearity gives (∑j=1ntjaj)⊗n=∑φtφ(1)⋯tφ(n) aφ(1)⊗⋯⊗aφ(n),\Bigl(\sum_{j=1}^n t_j a_j\Bigr)^{\otimes n} =\sum_\varphi t_{\varphi(1)}\cdots t_{\varphi(n)} \,a_{\varphi(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{\varphi(n)},(j=1∑n​tj​aj​)⊗n=φ∑​tφ(1)​⋯tφ(n)​aφ(1)​⊗⋯⊗aφ(n)​, where φ\varphiφ ranges over maps from {1,…,n}\{1,\dots,n\}{1,…,n} to itself. A term contributes to the coefficient of t1⋯tnt_1\cdots t_nt1​⋯tn​ exactly when each index occurs once. Thus the coefficient is the sum over the bijections, which is n!n!n! times the average of a1⊗⋯⊗ana_1\otimes\cdots\otimes a_na1​⊗⋯⊗an​.

  3. Passing from values to coefficients. A linear functional that vanishes on the span of the diagonal powers sends the displayed polynomial to a scalar polynomial vanishing on Cn\C^nCn. All coefficients are zero by induction on the number of variables, since a nonzero one-variable polynomial has finitely many roots. Hence the functional vanishes on every averaged simple tensor. If a vector were outside the span of the diagonal powers, we could extend a basis of the span by that vector and choose a functional zero on the span and 111 on the vector. Thus the averaged tensors lie in the span.

Summary

  • On V⊗nV^{\otimes n}V⊗n, the place and diagonal actions each span the other’s commutant (Corollary 31.8).
  • The occurring pairs are Vλ\Vlam{\lambda}Vλ and Sλ(V)\Schur{\lambda}{V}Sλ​(V) for ℓ(λ)≤d\len(\lambda)\le dℓ(λ)≤d. The second factor is irreducible with character sλ(x)s_\lambda(x)sλ​(x) and dimension equal to the pattern count (Theorem 31.11 and Corollary 31.14).
  • We began with actions and group structure (Lectures 1 to 10) and then studied characters (Lectures 11 to 14). Group algebras, induction and arithmetic (Lectures 15 to 21) provided new ways to construct representations. We classified the symmetric-group irreducibles and found their characters (Lectures 22 to 30), then related them to GL(V)\GL(V)GL(V) by Schur–Weyl duality (Theorem 31.11). Lie Theory continues with representations of Lie groups and Gelfand–Tsetlin bases, with integration taking the place of the finite average for compact groups. Modular representation theory studies fields of characteristic a prime dividing the group order, where Maschke’s theorem can fail (Example 11.13).
