Lecture 9

The arithmetic of characters

Character values are sums of roots of unity, so they are algebraic integers. This lecture turns that arithmetic into theorems about groups: every irreducible complex representation has degree dividing ∣G∣\abs{G}∣G∣. Burnside’s theorem connects group structure with representation theory: groups of order paqbp^aq^bpaqb are solvable. Last, the Frobenius–Schur indicator decides which invariant bilinear form an irreducible representation carries.

9.5 The Frobenius–Schur indicator

Summary

  • The main results: character values and central characters are algebraic integers, so every irreducible degree divides ∣G∣\abs{G}∣G∣ (Theorems 9.3 and 9.6). An algebraic-integer average of roots of unity is zero or all the roots averaged are equal (Lemma 9.11). A class of prime-power size greater than one gives a nontrivial proper normal subgroup; groups of order paqbp^aq^bpaqb, with distinct primes p,qp,qp,q and a,b≥0a,b\ge0a,b≥0, are solvable (Theorem 9.13). For an irreducible complex representation, the Frobenius–Schur indicator is 111 for a non-zero invariant symmetric form, −1-1−1 for an alternating one, and 000 when neither exists (Theorem 9.15).
