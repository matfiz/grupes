  • Exercise 1 Splitting classes and testing simplicity

(5,3,1),(3,3,1,1,1).(5,3,1),\qquad (3,3,1,1,1).(5,3,1),(3,3,1,1,1). Use the cycle-type class count from Theorem 6.4.   • Exercise 3 Two routes through a product

    Let G=S3×C2G=\SG3\times\Cyc2G=S3​×C2​ with C2={e,z}\Cyc2=\{e,z\}C2​={e,z}.

    1. Show that every nontrivial simple quotient of GGG has order two.

    2. Find all maximal normal subgroups as kernels of surjections onto C2\Cyc2C2​.

    3. Write MMM for the kernel of sign on the first factor and NNN for the kernel of the map nontrivial on both factors. Give a composition series through each of M,NM,NM,N, with the factors in order. For K=M∩NK=M\cap NK=M∩N, identify KKK and give the two quotient isomorphisms from the diamond in the proof of Theorem 9.10.

    Use sign from Lecture 6, direct products from Lecture 5 and the isomorphism theorems from Lecture 4.

  • Exercise 4 A refinement at order eighteen

    Write D9\Dih9D9​ with rotation rrr of order nine and reflection sss, and let xxx generate C18\Cyc{18}C18​. (i) Explain why D9⊵⟨r⟩⊵{e}\Dih9\nsup\langle r\rangle\nsup\{e\}D9​⊵⟨r⟩⊵{e} stops too soon for a composition series. Refine the chain. Compare its ordered factors and its composition factors with those of a series of C18\Cyc{18}C18​ through ⟨x3⟩\langle x^3\rangle⟨x3⟩ and then ⟨x6⟩\langle x^6\rangle⟨x6⟩.

    (ii) Prove that a proper normal subgroup of D9\Dih9D9​ contains no reflection. Deduce that the refined series in (i) is the only composition series of D9\Dih9D9​. Use the dihedral law from Lecture 5 and normality from Lecture 4.

  • Exercise 5 harder

    Prove that An\AG nAn​ is simple for every n≥5n\ge5n≥5. Use the base case Theorem 9.5 and the following induction reduction. If a nontrivial normal subgroup meets a point stabiliser nontrivially, simplicity of An−1\AG{n-1}An−1​ makes it contain that stabiliser. It then contains all point stabilisers and all three-cycles. You may use that the three-cycles generate An\AG nAn​.

    Show that a nonidentity element of a normal subgroup yields a nonidentity element fixing a point. Use conjugation of cycles from Lecture 6 and normality from Lecture 4. Treat n=6n=6n=6 separately if the element moves all six points.
