Simple groups and Jordan–Hölder

What this lecture does

A simple group has no proper nontrivial normal subgroup. We use conjugacy classes to prove that A5\AG{5}A5​ is simple. Then we break a finite group into simple quotients and show what those quotients have in common when we choose different chains.

Where we are

A simple group has no proper nontrivial normal subgroup and is itself nontrivial (Definition 8.3). Every normal subgroup is a union of conjugacy classes (Proposition 4.3), and its order divides the order of the group (Corollary 2.11). The classes in Sn\SG{n}Sn​ are the cycle types, and the class of type μ\muμ has n!/zμn!/\zmu\mun!/zμ​ elements (Theorem 6.4). In A4\AG{4}A4​ the 333-cycles form two classes of four (Example 8.9).

9.1 Classes of An\AG{n}An​ and simplicity of A5\AG{5}A5​

Before testing simplicity, we need to know how many even permutations we can reach by conjugating with even permutations alone. If an odd permutation commutes with σ\sigmaσ, we can multiply an odd conjugator on the right by that permutation. This changes the sign of the conjugator while leaving the conjugate of σ\sigmaσ unchanged.

Example 9.1 (Changing the parity of a conjugator). For σ=(1 2 3)\sigma=(1\,2\,3)σ=(123) in A5\AG{5}A5​, the odd permutation (1 2)(1\,2)(12) conjugates σ\sigmaσ to (1 3 2)(1\,3\,2)(132). The disjoint transposition (4 5)(4\,5)(45) commutes with σ\sigmaσ. Thus the even permutation (1 2)(4 5)(1\,2)(4\,5)(12)(45) gives the same conjugate. Both computations follow by relabelling the entries of the cycle (Theorem 6.4).

9.2 Composition series and Jordan–Hölder

A proper normal subgroup gives a smaller group and a quotient. If the quotient is simple, we can set it aside and continue with the smaller group. The successive quotients record what was removed at each step.

Definition 9.7 (Composition series). A composition series of GGG is a chain G=G0⊵G1⊵⋯⊵Gr=1G=G_0\nsup G_1\nsup\dots\nsup G_r=\trivG=G0​⊵G1​⊵⋯⊵Gr​=1 in which Gi+1G_{i+1}Gi+1​ is a proper normal subgroup of GiG_iGi​ and the factor Gi/Gi+1G_i/G_{i+1}Gi​/Gi+1​ is simple for each 0≤i<r0\le i<r0≤i<r. The number rrr is the length. Two composition series are equivalent if they have the same length and their factors can be matched by isomorphisms after reordering.

For S3\SG{3}S3​, the chain S3⊵A3⊵1\SG{3}\nsup\AG{3}\nsup\trivS3​⊵A3​⊵1 has simple factors C2\Cyc{2}C2​ and C3\Cyc{3}C3​ by their prime orders. For A5\AG{5}A5​, simplicity gives the series A5⊵1\AG{5}\nsup\trivA5​⊵1, with the non-abelian factor A5\AG{5}A5​ itself. The trivial group has a series of length zero.

Each step requires normality in the preceding group. In Example 5.6, an order-two subgroup is normal in the four-element subgroup but fails to be normal in the group above it.

A quotient can be split further if it has a proper nontrivial normal subgroup. By correspondence, such a subgroup comes from a normal subgroup strictly between the two groups at that step.

Definition 9.8 (Maximal normal subgroup). A proper normal subgroup NNN of GGG is maximal normal if there is no normal subgroup KKK of GGG with N<K<GN<K<GN<K<G.

For example, A3\AG{3}A3​ has index two in S3\SG{3}S3​, so Lagrange leaves no intermediate subgroup. Thus it is maximal normal.

Lemma 9.9 (Simple quotients). For N⊴GN\nsub GN⊴G, the quotient G/NG/NG/N is simple exactly when NNN is a maximal normal subgroup of GGG.

Proof. The correspondence theorem (Theorem 4.8) identifies normal subgroups of G/NG/NG/N with normal subgroups of GGG containing NNN. The endpoints are NNN and GGG, corresponding to the trivial subgroup and the whole quotient. The quotient is nontrivial exactly when N<GN<GN<G. In that case it is simple exactly when there is no intermediate normal subgroup of GGG.

Theorem 9.10 (Jordan–Hölder). Every finite group has a composition series. Any two composition series of the same finite group are equivalent.

Proof. We induct on the group order. For existence we choose a maximal normal subgroup. To compare two series, we use the intersection of their first proper terms to build a common tail.

  1. Existence. For 1\triv1, the chain of length zero is the only one, since there is no proper subgroup. In any other finite group GGG, the trivial subgroup is proper and normal. Choose a proper normal subgroup of largest order. It is maximal normal and hence gives a simple quotient by Lemma 9.9. Since it is smaller than GGG, induction gives it a composition series, which we append to the first step.

  2. The first terms. Let MMM and NNN be the first proper terms of two composition series of GGG. Both are maximal normal by Lemma 9.9. If M=NM=NM=N, induction compares the two tails in MMM, and both series have the same top factor G/MG/MG/M.

  3. Compare the quotients when M≠NM\ne NM=N. If N≤MN\le MN≤M, then N<M<GN<M<GN<M<G with MMM normal in GGG, contradicting maximality of NNN. The normal product MNMNMN therefore properly contains MMM, so maximality of MMM gives MN=GMN=GMN=G. Set K=M∩NK=M\cap NK=M∩N. Applying the second isomorphism theorem in both orders gives the two pairs of quotients in the right diagram of Figure 9.1. Summary

  • For n≥2n\ge2n≥2, an even Sn\SG{n}Sn​-class splits in An\AG{n}An​ exactly when its centraliser contains only even elements (Lemma 9.2).
  • The resulting class sizes prove that A5\AG{5}A5​ is simple (Theorem 9.5).
  • Maximal normal subgroups give simple quotients (Lemma 9.9). Jordan–Hölder uses the second isomorphism theorem (Theorem 4.7) to compare choices and proves that the factors, with their multiplicities, are independent of the series (Theorem 9.10).
