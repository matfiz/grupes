Part II — Representations and the Symmetric Group

Restriction and induction

What this lecture does

We move representations between a finite group and a subgroup. We first keep only the subgroup action and ask when repeated summands occur. To go in the other direction, we put a copy of the subgroup representation over each coset and build a group action.

Definition 17.3 (Multiplicity-free). A representation is multiplicity-free if each irreducible occurs with multiplicity at most one.

When the commutant is commutative.

By Theorem 16.8, End⁡H(Res⁡HGV)\End_H(\Res{G}{H}V)EndH​(ResHG​V) has a full matrix block of size mmm for each constituent with multiplicity m>0m>0m>0. A block of size one is commutative. 17.3 Induction and its character

The coset recipe of §17.2 depends on a transversal and leaves the action law to check by hand. The balanced tensor product needs no choice, gets the action law from left multiplication and gives the two-line argument for reciprocity in Lecture 18.

To represent the same vector in the coset slot, writing rhrhrh in the first factor must have the same effect as letting hhh act on the second.

Recall the vector-space tensor product of Definition 16.3 and its bilinear-map rule: a bilinear map on U×MU\times MU×M gives a linear map on U⊗MU\otimes MU⊗M with the same values on pure tensors. Here C[H]\grpalg{H}C[H] acts on WWW on the left by Proposition 15.5.
