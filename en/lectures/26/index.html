Part II — Representations and the Symmetric Group

The classification of the irreducibles of Sn\SG{n}Sn​

What this lecture does

The weight of a GZ line gives a standard tableau. We show that the tableaux of one shape are linked by adjacent swaps. Counting the shapes then gives one irreducible per shape, and taking away the last box describes restriction.

Lemma 26.3 (Admissible transpositions of tableaux). Let T∈SYT⁡(n)T\in\SYT(n)T∈SYT(n) and 1≤i<n1\le i<n1≤i<n, with axial distance rrr.

  2. Otherwise siTs_iTsi​T is standard of the same shape, α(siT)=siα(T)\alpha(s_iT)=s_i\alpha(T)α(si​T)=si​α(T) and ∣r∣≥2\abs r\ge2∣r∣≥2.

Thus sis_isi​ is admissible for α(T)\alpha(T)α(T) exactly when siTs_iTsi​T is standard.

Proof. We compare the order of the entries before and after the swap, then compute the distance from their box positions.

  1. One row or column. There is no integer strictly between iii and i+1i+1i+1. If they share a row, i+1i+1i+1 is therefore in the box immediately to the right of iii. If they share a column, it is immediately below iii. These positions give the claimed distances, and a swap reverses the increasing order.

  2. Different rows and columns. Every third entry is smaller than both or larger than both, so its comparison with each remains the same after the swap. The two exchanged entries share no row or column. The filling stays standard and the tuple of contents has the corresponding two entries exchanged.

  3. The distance across rows and columns. The box of i+1i+1i+1 cannot lie in a higher row and farther left than iii, since then its entry would be smaller by the increasing property in §25.3. It cannot be strictly below and right either. In that case the box immediately to the right of iii would exist and hold an entry strictly between iii and i+1i+1i+1. Thus if i+1i+1i+1 is in a higher row it is farther right, and if it is in a lower row it is farther left. The changes in row and column then have opposite signs. Subtracting the row change from the column change gives absolute value at least two.

26.2 Connectivity and the classification

The move in Example 26.2 is reversible since it just exchanges the same two entries. Two content vectors are linked if a chain of admissible swaps, each applied to the current tuple, takes one to the other. This is an equivalence relation. The empty chain gives reflexivity, reversing a chain gives symmetry, and joining chains gives transitivity.

To link several tableaux, it helps to choose one filling to which we can move all the others.

Definition 26.4 (Row tableau). For λ⊢n\lambda\vdashn nλ⊢n, the row tableau TλT^\lambdaTλ fills the first row with 1,…,λ11,\dots,\lambda_11,…,λ1​, the second row with the next λ2\lambda_2λ2​ numbers, and so on.

The filling is standard because each row increases and every entry is larger than all those in higher rows. For example, the right-hand square tableau in Example 26.2 has 444 in the last box of the last row, as does the row tableau on the left. Removing that box leaves the two fillings The move s2s_2s2​ connects them with 444 fixed. In general we first arrange for the last box of the last row to hold nnn.

Starting from any weight of an irreducible, we can stay inside it and reach an entire linked class. To decide how many linked classes that irreducible contains, we compare the number of shapes with the number of irreducibles.

Theorem 26.7 (The classification). For every n≥1n\ge1n≥1, Spec⁡(n)=Cont⁡(n)\Spec(n)=\Cont(n)Spec(n)=Cont(n). For each λ⊢n\lambda\vdashn nλ⊢n, exactly one irreducible of Sn\SG{n}Sn​ has as its weights the content vectors of shape λ\lambdaλ. This gives a bijection between partitions and irreducibles.

Proof. We assign each irreducible a shape and use the count to see that every shape is assigned exactly once.

  1. All tuples of one shape occur in VVV. Take any weight of the irreducible VVV. It is a content vector by Theorem 25.6. Admissible swaps stay inside VVV by Theorem 24.3 and reach every content vector of this shape by Theorem 26.5.

  2. Counting shapes. Choose one such shape λV\lambda_VλV​ for each VVV. Two irreducibles with the same chosen shape would share a weight, for example that of its row tableau. Weight separation (Corollary 23.5) makes them the same irreducible. The choice V↦λVV\mapsto\lambda_VV↦λV​ is therefore injective. Completeness counts the irreducibles by classes (Theorem 14.4), and cycle type counts the classes by partitions (Theorem 6.4): #Irr⁡(Sn)=#conjugacy classes of Sn=p(n).\#\Irr(\SG{n})=\#\text{conjugacy classes of }\SG{n}=p(n).#Irr(Sn​)=#conjugacy classes of Sn​=p(n). The injection is a bijection.

  3. No further shape in one irreducible. If VVV had a weight of a second shape μ\muμ, that weight would also occur in the irreducible assigned μ\muμ. Separation excludes this. Each shape is assigned and all of its tuples occur, so every content vector is a weight. The reverse inclusion is Theorem 25.6.

26.3 Removing a box

Truncating the path of a weight drops its last entry. In the tableau this drops its last box, so the shape of the remaining tableau should name the smaller irreducible.

Corollary 26.10 (One-box restriction). For λ⊢n\lambda\vdashn nλ⊢n with n≥2n\ge2n≥2, Res⁡Sn−1SnVλ≅⨁μ=λ−□Vμ,\Res{\SG{n}}{\SG{n-1}}\Vlam{\lambda} \cong\bigoplus_{\mu=\lambda-\square}\Vlam{\mu},ResSn−1​Sn​​Vλ≅μ=λ−□⨁​Vμ, where the sum is over the partitions obtained by removing one box. The summand for μ\muμ contains precisely the GZ lines whose tableaux have shape μ\muμ after deleting nnn.

Proof. We find the summand for each path and then realise every allowed removal.

  1. The last edge. For a tableau TTT of shape λ\lambdaλ, let T′T'T′ be obtained by deleting its box of nnn. The path to vTv_TvT​ passes through an irreducible of Sn−1\SG{n-1}Sn−1​. This is the content vector of T′T'T′, so its shape names that irreducible by Theorem 26.7.

  2. Every removal. Given a shape μ=λ−□\mu=\lambda-\squareμ=λ−□, append nnn in the removed box to any standard tableau of shape μ\muμ. The restriction is multiplicity-free by Theorem 22.4, so each of these summands occurs once.

Summary

  • An admissible swap exchanges consecutive entries in different rows and columns. Such swaps connect all the tableaux of a fixed shape (Lemma 26.3 and Theorem 26.5).
  • Each shape gives one irreducible of Sn\SG{n}Sn​. Its GZ lines and its degree are indexed and counted by its standard tableaux (Theorem 26.7 and Corollary 26.8).
  • Restriction removes one box with multiplicity one, so the branching graph is Young’s graph (Corollary 26.10 and Definition 26.11). Its edges record the entries of a weight.
