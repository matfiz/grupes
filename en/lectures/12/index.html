Part I — Groups and Characters

Schur’s lemma and multiplicities

What this lecture does

A representation can split in several ways. We find what every splitting has in common by studying maps from an irreducible representation into the whole space. We also use these maps to find the irreducibles of a cyclic group.

Where we are

When the group order is invertible in the field, Maschke’s theorem splits a finite-dimensional representation into irreducibles (Theorem 11.11). An intertwiner commutes with the action in the sense of Definition 11.3. In the three-point action, C3=L⊕E\C^3=L\oplus EC3=L⊕E, where LLL is the constant line and the plane EEE consists of vectors with coordinate sum zero (Example 11.5). We work over C\CC unless a field is named.

The calculation on EEE suggests that irreducibility can force every intertwining endomorphism to be scalar. The general argument starts with the kernel and image, and uses an eigenvalue to find a kernel.

A field is algebraically closed if every nonconstant polynomial over it has a root in it. The fundamental theorem of algebra gives this property for C\CC.

12.2 Counting copies of an irreducible

In C3=L⊕E\C^3=L\oplus EC3=L⊕E, a map from EEE to C3\C^3C3 can be read in two components. Schur’s lemma makes the component in LLL zero and the component in EEE scalar. This gives one dimension of maps, corresponding to one copy of EEE. We use this dimension to count copies without choosing a decomposition.

12.3 Abelian groups and roots of unity

When the group is abelian, each action operator commutes with every other operator. It is then itself an intertwiner. We can use Schur’s lemma before solving any equations for the operators.

Corollary 12.6 (Abelian irreducibles and eigenvalues). Every irreducible complex representation of a finite abelian group is one-dimensional. For any complex representation of a finite group and any g∈Gg\in Gg∈G, the operator ρ(g)\rho(g)ρ(g) is diagonalisable with eigenvalues that are o(g)o(g)o(g)th roots of unity.

Proof. First consider an abelian group acting on an irreducible. Then apply that conclusion to the powers of one element.

  1. An abelian group. For each h∈Gh\in Gh∈G, the operator ρ(h)\rho(h)ρ(h) commutes with every ρ(g)\rho(g)ρ(g) by commutativity in GGG. By the scalar conclusion of Theorem 12.2, every ρ(h)\rho(h)ρ(h) is scalar. Every line is invariant, so irreducibility forces dimension one.

  2. One element. Let only the cyclic subgroup ⟨g⟩\langle g\rangle⟨g⟩ act on the space. Maschke’s theorem and the abelian case split that action into lines. A basis of the lines diagonalises ρ(g)\rho(g)ρ(g), and ρ(g)o(g)=11\rho(g)^{o(g)}=\Idρ(g)o(g)=11 makes each eigenvalue an o(g)o(g)o(g)th root of unity.

 

The diagonalisation also follows from Theorem 11.11. In an invariant inner product every action operator is unitary, so the spectral theorem for normal operators gives a basis of eigenvectors. Over R\RR, the abelian conclusion already fails for the rotation in Example 12.3.

Example 12.7 (The cyclic group). Let Cn=⟨g⟩\Cyc{n}=\langle g\rangleCn​=⟨g⟩ and ζ=exp⁡(2πi/n)\zeta=\exp(2\pi\ii/n)ζ=exp(2πi/n). Every complex irreducible is a line by Corollary 12.6. Summary

  • An intertwiner between irreducibles is zero or an isomorphism. Over an algebraically closed field, intertwining endomorphisms of an irreducible are scalar (Theorem 12.2).
  • Over C\CC, the multiplicity of UUU in VVV is dim⁡Hom⁡G(U,V)\dim\Hom_G(U,V)dimHomG​(U,V). These dimensions determine VVV up to isomorphism (Corollary 12.5).
  • Complex irreducibles of a finite abelian group are lines. For Cn\Cyc{n}Cn​, each nnnth root of unity for the generator gives one irreducible (Corollary 12.6 and Example 12.7).

4 Let UUU and WWW be nonisomorphic complex irreducible representations of dimension 333. What rank can a nonzero, noninvertible intertwining endomorphism of U⊕WU\oplus WU⊕W have?

Here is the hint — try once more. Write the map in four blocks and use Schur’s lemma on both the diagonal and the off-diagonal blocks. Schur’s lemma (Theorem 12.2) makes the off-diagonal blocks zero and the diagonal blocks scalar identities. Exactly one scalar is nonzero for a nonzero, noninvertible map, so its image is one three-dimensional summand and its rank is 333.
