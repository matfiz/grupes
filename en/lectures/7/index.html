Lecture 7

The group algebra

What this lecture does

Lecture 6 showed that GGG has at most as many irreducible representations as conjugacy classes. The group algebra C[G]\grpalg{G}C[G] contains every irreducible representation and explains why their number equals the number of classes. It is isomorphic to the direct sum of the matrix algebras End⁡(U)\End(U)End(U), and its centre counts the classes. The matrix blocks End⁡(U)\End(U)End(U) also compute the commutant End⁡G(V)\End_G(V)EndG​(V), and decide when restriction to a subgroup is multiplicity-free, as Lecture 10 needs for Sn\SG{n}Sn​.

7.1 Algebras and modules

Throughout, GGG is finite, and vector spaces are complex and finite-dimensional.

Definition 7.1 (Algebras). An algebra is a vector space AAA with a bilinear, associative multiplication (a,b)↦ab(a,b)\mapsto ab(a,b)↦ab that has an identity element. A subalgebra is a subspace closed under multiplication and containing the identity. An algebra homomorphism is a linear map φ ⁣:A→B\varphi\colon A\to Bφ:A→B with φ(ab)=φ(a)φ(b)\varphi(ab)=\varphi(a)\varphi(b)φ(ab)=φ(a)φ(b) that sends the identity to the identity.

The endomorphism space End⁡(V)\End(V)End(V) is an algebra under composition, with identity 11\Id11. In a basis it is the matrix algebra Mn(C)M_n(\C)Mn​(C), where n=dim⁡Vn=\dim Vn=dimV. The direct sum A⊕BA\oplus BA⊕B of two algebras has componentwise operations.

Definition 7.2 (The group algebra). The group algebra C[G]\grpalg{G}C[G] is the vector space with basis GGG, whose elements are the formal sums a=∑g∈Gag ga=\sum_{g\in G}a_g\,ga=∑g∈G​ag​g with ag∈Ca_g\in\Cag​∈C, and with the multiplication of GGG extended bilinearly: (∑gag g)(∑hbh h)=∑g,hagbh gh.\Bigl(\sum_{g}a_g\,g\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{h}b_h\,h\Bigr)=\sum_{g,h}a_gb_h\,gh.(g∑​ag​g)(h∑​bh​h)=g,h∑​ag​bh​gh.

The group algebra is associative because GGG is. Its identity is eee, and dim⁡C[G]=∣G∣\dim\grpalg{G}=\abs{G}dimC[G]=∣G∣. It is commutative exactly when GGG is abelian.

Example 7.3 (The group algebra of C2\Cyc{2}C2​). Let C2={e,t}\Cyc{2}=\{e,t\}C2​={e,t}, with t2=et^2=et2=e. An element of its group algebra is a formal sum ae+btae+btae+bt with a,b∈Ca,b\in\Ca,b∈C. Bilinearity gives (ae+bt)(ce+dt)=(ac+bd)e+(ad+bc)t.(ae+bt)(ce+dt)=(ac+bd)e+(ad+bc)t.(ae+bt)(ce+dt)=(ac+bd)e+(ad+bc)t. The basis elements eee and ttt are distinct vectors, even though eee also serves as the identity for multiplication.

Definition 7.4 (Modules). A module over an algebra AAA is a vector space VVV with an algebra homomorphism ρ ⁣:A→End⁡(V)\rho\colon A\to\End(V)ρ:A→End(V); we write a⋅v=ρ(a)va\cdot v=\rho(a)va⋅v=ρ(a)v. 7.2 The regular module and the matrix blocks

For G=C2={e,t}G=\Cyc{2}=\{e,t\}G=C2​={e,t}, left multiplication by ttt exchanges the basis vectors eee and ttt.
