Lecture 1

Groups and symmetries

This course studies finite groups and how they act by linear maps. In Part I, we first study groups through the sets they move. We then turn to actions on vector spaces and begin to read information about a group from its character table. Part II continues that study. We begin by composing symmetries of a triangle.

1.1 Groups

Centre an equilateral triangle at the origin and number its vertices anticlockwise. A symmetry is an orthogonal map of the plane that carries the triangle to itself. The arrow in Figure 1.1 shows the rotation rrr through 2π/32\pi/32π/3. The dashed line is the axis of the reflection sss.

The rotation sends 111 to 222, 222 to 333 and 333 to 111. The reflection fixes vertex 111 and exchanges vertices 222 and 333. To compute rsrsrs, apply sss first. Follow each vertex through the two maps. 1↦s1↦r2,2↦s3↦r1,3↦s2↦r3.\begin{equation} \label{eq:1:rs} 1\xmapsto{s}1\xmapsto{r}2,\qquad 2\xmapsto{s}3\xmapsto{r}1,\qquad 3\xmapsto{s}2\xmapsto{r}3. \end{equation}1s​1r​2,2s​3r​1,3s​2r​3.(1.1) Thus rsrsrs exchanges vertices 111 and 222. A composition of two symmetries is again a symmetry. We can also leave the triangle in place, or undo any symmetry. The group axioms record these rules for a product.

Definition 1.1 (Group). A group is a set GGG with a product G×G→GG\times G\to GG×G→G, (g,h)↦gh(g,h)\mapsto gh(g,h)↦gh, with the following properties.

  • The product is associative. For all g,h,k∈Gg,h,k\in Gg,h,k∈G, (gh)k=g(hk)(gh)k=g(hk)(gh)k=g(hk).

  • There is an identity e∈Ge\in Ge∈G, with eg=ge=geg=ge=geg=ge=g for all g∈Gg\in Gg∈G.

  • Each g∈Gg\in Gg∈G has an inverse g−1∈Gg^{-1}\in Gg−1∈G, with gg−1=g−1g=egg^{-1}=g^{-1}g=egg−1=g−1g=e.

The order ∣G∣\abs{G}∣G∣ of GGG is its number of elements. The group GGG is abelian if gh=hggh=hggh=hg for all g,h∈Gg,h\in Gg,h∈G.

The triangle symmetries satisfy the axioms. Composition of maps is associative. The identity map preserves the triangle, and the inverse of an orthogonal map preserving the triangle preserves it too. The vertices are the points of the triangle farthest from its centre, so a symmetry permutes them. Its values on the independent vertex vectors at 111 and 222 determine it. Hence the group is finite.

The triangle group is non-abelian. The product rsrsrs sends 111 to 222. To find where srsrsr sends 111, rotate it to 222 and then reflect it to 333. Changing which factor acts first changes the image of this vertex.

The axioms give a unique identity and a unique inverse for each element. If eee and e′e'e′ are identities, then e=ee′=e′e=ee'=e'e=ee′=e′. The first equality uses the identity property of e′e'e′ and the second uses that of eee. If hhh and h′h'h′ are inverses of ggg, then h=h(gh′)=(hg)h′=h′.h=h(gh')=(hg)h'=h'.h=h(gh′)=(hg)h′=h′. Here gh′=egh'=egh′=e and hg=ehg=ehg=e, and associativity gives the middle equality.

To undo a product of two symmetries, undo the last symmetry applied first. The same rule follows from the axioms in any group. The products in both orders are (gh)(h−1g−1)=g(hh−1)g−1=e,(h−1g−1)(gh)=h−1(g−1g)h=e.\begin{aligned} (gh)(h^{-1}g^{-1})&=g(hh^{-1})g^{-1}=e,\\ (h^{-1}g^{-1})(gh)&=h^{-1}(g^{-1}g)h=e. \end{aligned}(gh)(h−1g−1)(h−1g−1)(gh)​=g(hh−1)g−1=e,=h−1(g−1g)h=e.​ Thus (gh)−1=h−1g−1(gh)^{-1}=h^{-1}g^{-1}(gh)−1=h−1g−1.

Repeated products of one element need less notation. For k≥1k\ge1k≥1, let gkg^kgk be the product of kkk copies of ggg. Put g0=eg^0=eg0=e. The reverse-order rule shows that the inverse of gkg^kgk is the product of kkk copies of g−1g^{-1}g−1, so we write g−k=(g−1)kg^{-k}=(g^{-1})^kg−k=(g−1)k.

For example, two turns by rrr send vertex 111 to 333, and a third completes the turn. The square r2r^2r2 therefore also undoes rrr. In general gjgk=gj+kg^jg^k=g^{j+k}gjgk=gj+k and (gj)k=gjk(g^j)^k=g^{jk}(gj)k=gjk for integers j,kj,kj,k. For positive exponents these laws follow by concatenating copies of ggg. For mixed signs, adjacent copies of ggg and g−1g^{-1}g−1 cancel. For example, g3g−2=g2(gg−1)g−1=g.g^3g^{-2}=g^2(gg^{-1})g^{-1}=g.g3g−2=g2(gg−1)g−1=g. 1.2 Groups of permutations

The products of triangle symmetries can be computed from their vertex images. We can also start with a set of points and ask what happens when we allow every bijection of that set.

Definition 1.2 (Symmetric group). The symmetric group SXS_XSX​ of a set XXX is the set of all bijections X→XX\to XX→X, with composition as the product. For X={1,…,n}X=\{1,\dots,n\}X={1,…,n} we write Sn\SG{n}Sn​ and call its elements permutations.

Composition is associative and the identity map id⁡\idid belongs to SXS_XSX​. The composite of bijections is a bijection, and the inverse map of a bijection is again a bijection. This verifies the group axioms.

For SnS_nSn​, the image of 111 has nnn choices and the image of 222 has n−1n-1n−1. Continue until the last unused point is forced. This gives ∣Sn∣=n!\abs{\SG{n}}=n!∣Sn​∣=n!. In particular the three points of the triangle have six permutations. We will match each of these to a symmetry in the worked example.

A cycle records a permutation by giving successive images. The cycle (a1 a2 … ak)(a_1\,a_2\,\dots\,a_k)(a1​a2​…ak​), with distinct entries, sends a1↦a2↦⋯↦ak↦a1a_1\mapsto a_2\mapsto\cdots\mapsto a_k\mapsto a_1a1​↦a2​↦⋯↦ak​↦a1​ and fixes all other points. Such a cycle is a kkk-cycle, and a 222-cycle is a transposition. For instance, (1 2)(1\,2)(12) sends 111 to 222 and 222 to 111. A one-cycle (i)(i)(i) fixes iii, so it may be omitted. In S3\SG{3}S3​, (1 2)(3)(1\,2)(3)(12)(3) and (1 2)(1\,2)(12) denote the same permutation.

Cycles are disjoint if they have no entry in common. The cycles (1 2 3)(1\,2\,3)(123) and (4 5)(4\,5)(45) are disjoint in S5\SG{5}S5​. Proposition 6.2 proves that every permutation is a product of disjoint cycles.

1.3 Subgroups and element order

The powers of rrr describe the symmetries we can obtain by repeating the same turn or its inverse. They form a group using the original product. The next definition specifies when a subset can be used in this way.

Definition 1.4 (Subgroup). A subgroup H≤GH\le GH≤G is a subset of GGG that contains eee and is closed under products and inverses. It is proper, written H<GH<GH<G, if H≠GH\ne GH=G. The trivial subgroup is 1={e}\triv=\{e\}1={e}. The subgroup generated by a subset S⊆GS\subseteq GS⊆G, written ⟨S⟩\langle S\rangle⟨S⟩, is the smallest subgroup containing SSS. We write ⟨g⟩\langle g\rangle⟨g⟩ for ⟨{g}⟩\langle\{g\}\rangle⟨{g}⟩.

The subset {e}\{e\}{e} is a subgroup since the product and inverse of the identity give the identity. The whole group GGG is also a subgroup of itself.

Intersect all the subgroups containing SSS. There is at least one, namely GGG. The intersection contains eee and is closed under products and inverses because each of those subgroups has these properties. Hence the intersection is the smallest subgroup containing SSS.

Proposition 1.5 (Products of generators). For a subset SSS of a group GGG, the subgroup ⟨S⟩\langle S\rangle⟨S⟩ consists of all finite products of elements of SSS and their inverses, including the empty product eee.

Proof. These products contain eee and are closed under concatenation and inversion. They therefore form a subgroup containing SSS. Every subgroup containing SSS contains each of these products. 

In particular, the powers {gk:k∈Z}\{g^k:k\in\mathbb Z\}{gk:k∈Z} form a subgroup by the power laws. The power with exponent zero supplies the identity. The product of two powers adds their exponents, and inversion negates the exponent. Every subgroup containing ggg must contain the positive powers by closure under products and the negative ones by closure under inverses. Thus this subgroup is ⟨g⟩\langle g\rangle⟨g⟩.

Example 1.6 (The powers of a turn and a reflection). In the triangle group, three turns by rrr give the identity, while the first two turns send vertex 111 to different vertices from its starting point. A reflection squares to the identity and sss exchanges vertices 222 and 333. It follows that ⟨r⟩={e,r,r2},⟨s⟩={e,s}.\langle r\rangle=\{e,r,r^2\},\qquad \langle s\rangle=\{e,s\}.⟨r⟩={e,r,r2},⟨s⟩={e,s}. Both subgroups are proper. The reflection is missing from the first, and the turn is missing from the second.

For any ggg in a finite group some two non-negative powers agree. If gi=gjg^i=g^jgi=gj with i<ji<ji<j, multiplying by g−ig^{-i}g−i gives gj−i=eg^{j-i}=egj−i=e. We can ask when the first return occurs and whether the powers of one element exhaust the whole group.

Definition 1.7 (Order of an element, cyclic group). The order o(g)o(g)o(g) of an element ggg of a finite group is the least integer k≥1k\ge1k≥1 with gk=eg^k=egk=e. A group GGG is cyclic if G=⟨g⟩G=\langle g\rangleG=⟨g⟩ for some g∈Gg\in Gg∈G. Such an element is a generator of GGG.

The first return calculations above give o(r)=3o(r)=3o(r)=3 and o(s)=2o(s)=2o(s)=2. The subgroup ⟨r⟩\langle r\rangle⟨r⟩ is cyclic, with generator rrr. When computing a longer sequence of powers of rrr, each full turn of the triangle lets us return to the same three powers. For instance, five turns give the same symmetry as two. Division with remainder works in the same way for every finite group.

The powers from eee to go(g)−1g^{o(g)-1}go(g)−1 are distinct. If two agreed, their exponent difference would give a positive return smaller than o(g)o(g)o(g). For m≥0m\ge0m≥0, divide mmm by o(g)o(g)o(g) to write m=q o(g)+tm=q\,o(g)+tm=qo(g)+t with 0≤t<o(g)0\le t<o(g)0≤t<o(g). Then gm=gtg^m=g^tgm=gt. The listed powers are closed under products by the same reduction. For an exponent 0<i<o(g)0<i<o(g)0<i<o(g), the inverse of gig^igi is go(g)−ig^{o(g)-i}go(g)−i. The inverse of eee is eee. Hence the listed powers form ⟨g⟩\langle g\rangle⟨g⟩, and their number is the order of ggg. ∣⟨g⟩∣=o(g).\begin{equation} \label{eq:1:generated-order} \abs{\langle g\rangle}=o(g). \end{equation}∣⟨g⟩∣=o(g).(1.2)

1.4 Homomorphisms and cyclic models

A symmetry of the triangle can be recorded as a permutation of its vertices. We want this record to preserve products. The permutation of a composite should be the composite of the recorded permutations.

Definition 1.8 (Homomorphism). A homomorphism φ ⁣:G→H\varphi\colon G\to Hφ:G→H between groups satisfies φ(gh)=φ(g)φ(h)\varphi(gh)=\varphi(g)\varphi(h)φ(gh)=φ(g)φ(h) for all g,h∈Gg,h\in Gg,h∈G. Its kernel is ker⁡φ={g∈G:φ(g)=e}\ker\varphi=\{g\in G:\varphi(g)=e\}kerφ={g∈G:φ(g)=e}, with eee the identity of HHH. Its image is im⁡φ={φ(g):g∈G}\im\varphi=\{\varphi(g):g\in G\}imφ={φ(g):g∈G}. A bijective homomorphism is an isomorphism. Groups with an isomorphism between them are isomorphic, written G≅HG\cong HG≅H.

On the rotation subgroup of the triangle, recording vertex images sends rrr to (1 2 3)(1\,2\,3)(123) and sends r2r^2r2 to (1 3 2)(1\,3\,2)(132). The second permutation is the square of the first. Only eee leaves all vertex labels unchanged, so the kernel is 1\triv1. The image is ⟨(1 2 3)⟩\langle(1\,2\,3)\rangle⟨(123)⟩. An isomorphism lets us read the same products in either group.

Every homomorphism preserves the identity. Indeed, φ(e)=φ(e)φ(e)\varphi(e)=\varphi(e)\varphi(e)φ(e)=φ(e)φ(e), and cancellation gives φ(e)=e\varphi(e)=eφ(e)=e. It also preserves inverses, since φ(g)φ(g−1)=φ(e)=e\varphi(g)\varphi(g^{-1})=\varphi(e)=eφ(g)φ(g−1)=φ(e)=e. The kernel is consequently closed under products and inverses, and contains eee. A product or inverse in the image is the image of the corresponding product or inverse in GGG, so the image is a subgroup.

Remark 1.9 (Injectivity and the kernel). A group homomorphism φ\varphiφ is injective exactly when its kernel is 1\triv1. To see this, compare two inputs with the same image. Since φ(g−1h)=φ(g)−1φ(h)\varphi(g^{-1}h)=\varphi(g)^{-1}\varphi(h)φ(g−1h)=φ(g)−1φ(h), we have φ(g)=φ(h)  ⟺  g−1h∈ker⁡φ.\varphi(g)=\varphi(h)\iff g^{-1}h\in\ker\varphi.φ(g)=φ(h)⟺g−1h∈kerφ. If the kernel is 1\triv1 then equal images force g=hg=hg=h. Conversely, injectivity forces eee to be the only element with image eee.

Example 1.10 (Cyclic and dihedral groups). Two families of groups come from rotations. The first records multiplication of complex numbers. The second also allows reflections of a polygon.

  • The nnnth roots of unity are the complex numbers zzz with zn=1z^n=1zn=1, for an integer n≥1n\ge1n≥1. Written as points on the unit circle, they are Cn={exp⁡(2πik/n):k=0,…,n−1}.\Cyc{n}=\{\exp(2\pi\ii k/n):k=0,\dots,n-1\}.Cn​={exp(2πik/n):k=0,…,n−1}. Every root is a power of the generator g=exp⁡(2πi/n)g=\exp(2\pi\ii/n)g=exp(2πi/n), so Cn\Cyc{n}Cn​ is cyclic of order nnn. For n=3n=3n=3 the generator is a turn through the same angle as rrr.

    The rotation sends kkk to k+1k+1k+1, and reflection sends kkk to 2−k2-k2−k. Applying the three factors sends vertex kkk to 2−(3−k)=k−12-(3-k)=k-12−(3−k)=k−1, as r−1r^{-1}r−1 does. The roots of unity provide a model for any finite cyclic group. First build the map for the rotation subgroup. Match rrr to exp⁡(2πi/3)\exp(2\pi\ii/3)exp(2πi/3) and match each power of rrr to the same power of this root. The exponents 111 and 444 name the same rotation because three turns give the identity. They also name the same root because the generator cubed is 111. Before we can use this rule as a map, we must know that choosing a different exponent never changes the image.

Proposition 1.11 (The model for finite cyclic groups). Every cyclic group of finite order nnn is isomorphic to Cn\Cyc{n}Cn​.

Proof. Let G=⟨g⟩G=\langle g\rangleG=⟨g⟩ have order nnn. The order of ggg is also nnn. We first check that equality of powers agrees in the two groups, then check bijectivity and products.

  1. Equality of powers. For 0≤j≤k0\le j\le k0≤j≤k, the equation gj=gkg^j=g^kgj=gk holds exactly when gk−j=eg^{k-j}=egk−j=e, hence when nnn divides k−jk-jk−j.   2. Bijectivity. Equality of the images forces equality of the powers by the same calculation. The map is onto since both sets have nnn elements.

  3. Products. The product of the exponentials for jjj and kkk has exponent 2πi(j+k)/n2\pi\ii(j+k)/n2πi(j+k)/n. This is the image of gj+kg^{j+k}gj+k, the product of gjg^jgj and gkg^kgk.

Summary

  • The triangle’s symmetries give D3≅S3\Dih{3}\cong\SG{3}D3​≅S3​ by their vertex permutations (Example 1.12). In a product of permutations the right factor acts first.
  • The subgroup generated by SSS consists of products of its elements and their inverses (Proposition 1.5). The powers of a single element ggg form a subgroup of size o(g)o(g)o(g) (Equation (1.2)).
  • Every finite cyclic group of order nnn is isomorphic to the nnnth roots of unity (Proposition 1.11).

Answer to the self-check

On {1,2,3}\{1,2,3\}{1,2,3} the product acts as (1 2 3)(1\,2\,3)(123), because (4 5)(4\,5)(45) fixes each of these points. A power fixes all three exactly when the exponent is a multiple of 333. On {4,5}\{4,5\}{4,5} the product acts as the swap (4 5)(4\,5)(45), so a power fixes both points exactly when the exponent is even. The first positive exponent meeting both conditions is 666, the order of the product.
