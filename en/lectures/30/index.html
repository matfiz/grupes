30.2 Identifying the characters

The same operation of removing a box occurs for Schur functions and for irreducible characters. We compare the two lists of smaller shapes before using them to recover a label.

A predecessor is the shape left after removing one box. If a shape has two different removals, the union of the two smaller diagrams recovers all its boxes. For shapes with one removal, we need a separate argument.

Lemma 30.7 (Predecessors). For n≥3n\ge3n≥3, a partition of nnn is determined by its set of one-box predecessors. For n=2n=2n=2, the shapes (2)(2)(2) and (1,1)(1,1)(1,1) both have only (1)(1)(1) as predecessor.

Proof. For n=2n=2n=2 each diagram has just one removable box and the stated predecessor. For n≥3n\ge3n≥3, suppose two distinct diagrams have the same predecessors. We use their intersection to identify the possible shapes, then count its boxes.

  1. The intersection. Choose one common predecessor μ\muμ. The two diagrams are obtained by adding different boxes to μ\muμ, so their intersection is μ\muμ. Every common predecessor has n−1n-1n−1 boxes and lies in this intersection. Thus μ\muμ is the only one.

  2. Rectangles. For each row length, the lowest row of that length ends in a removable box. A single removable box means all rows have the same length. Write the two rectangles as (ab)(a^b)(ab) and (cd)(c^d)(cd). Since ab=cd=nab=cd=nab=cd=n and the rectangles are distinct, we can order the widths as a<ca<ca<c, and then b>db>db>d.

30.3 The Frobenius formula

The Hall product with a single power sum pμp_\mupμ​ selects one character value. Extraction computes this product by picking one monomial. If the shape has few rows, only a few variables are needed.

Corollary 30.9 (The Frobenius formula). For μ⊢n\mu\vdashn nμ⊢n and N≥nN\ge nN≥n, the Frobenius expansion is pμ=∑λ⊢nχλ(μ)sλ.p_\mu=\sum_{\lambda\vdashn n}\chil{\lambda}(\mu)s_\lambda .pμ​=λ⊢n∑​χλ(μ)sλ​. For any partition λ⊢n\lambda\vdashn nλ⊢n, the coefficient form holds in N≥ℓ(λ)N\ge\len(\lambda)N≥ℓ(λ) variables: χλ(μ)=[xλ+δ](aδpμ).\chil{\lambda}(\mu)=\coeff{x^{\lambda+\delta}}(a_\delta p_\mu).χλ(μ)=[xλ+δ](aδ​pμ​).

Proof. We compute in the Hall basis first, then compare the coefficient before and after dropping a variable.

  1. At least nnn variables. With conjugation in the first slot, the characteristic and extraction give χλ(μ)‾=⟨ch⁡(χλ),pμ⟩=⟨sλ,pμ⟩=[xλ+δ](aδpμ).\begin{aligned} \overline{\chil{\lambda}(\mu)} &=\inner{\ch(\chil{\lambda})}{p_\mu}\\ &=\inner{s_\lambda}{p_\mu}\\ &=\coeff{x^{\lambda+\delta}}(a_\delta p_\mu). \end{aligned}χλ(μ)​​=⟨ch(χλ),pμ​⟩=⟨sλ​,pμ​⟩=[xλ+δ](aδ​pμ​).​ The second line uses Theorem 30.8, and the last uses Lemma 30.1.   2. Fewer variables. Write δM\delta_MδM​ for the staircase in MMM variables as in Lemma 29.11. For N≥ℓ(λ)N\ge\len(\lambda)N≥ℓ(λ) the last entry of λ+δN+1\lambda+\delta_{N+1}λ+δN+1​ is zero, so setting xN+1=0x_{N+1}=0xN+1​=0 keeps the target coefficient.

    The power sum products specialise to themselves, and the staircase product of Lemma 29.3 gives aδN+1(x1,…,xN,0)=x1⋯xN aδN(x1,…,xN).a_{\delta_{N+1}}(x_1,\dots,x_N,0) =x_1\cdots x_N\,a_{\delta_N}(x_1,\dots,x_N).aδN+1​​(x1​,…,xN​,0)=x1​⋯xN​aδN​​(x1​,…,xN​). Summary

  • Multiplying by the staircase alternant extracts a Schur coefficient. Applied to characteristics, extraction gives the integrality of the inverse Schur functions (Lemma 30.1 and Corollary 30.4).
  • Integrality, norm and positivity give irreducible characters, and one-box restriction matches their labels (Theorem 30.8).
  • The Frobenius expansion of pμp_\mupμ​ in N≥nN\ge nN≥n variables has coefficients χλ(μ)\chil{\lambda}(\mu)χλ(μ) (Corollary 30.9). The trace on a tensor power uses this expansion in Theorem 31.11.
