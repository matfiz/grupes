Lecture 24

The local structure and the braid exclusion

An adjacent transposition can mix two Gelfand–Tsetlin lines. We find which two lines it can mix and how much of the original vector it retains. Then we use adjacent transpositions together to rule out certain weights. These constraints help determine Spec⁡(n)\Spec(n)Spec(n), the set of all weights of Sn\SG{n}Sn​. Lectures 25 and 26 complete that determination.

Where we are

The eigenvalues of the Jucys–Murphy elements on a GZ line form its weight (Definition 22.10), and the weights distinguish all paths (Corollary 23.5). When vvv lies on a path line, the span of vvv and sivs_i vsi​v is invariant under sis_isi​ and the Xk\JM{k}Xk​ (Corollary 23.9).

Lemma 24.2 (GZ vectors and the inner product).

  1. The operators sis_isi​ and Xk\JM{k}Xk​ on VVV are self-adjoint, and the weight entries are real.

  2. The GZ lines of VVV are pairwise orthogonal.

  3. A nonzero joint eigenvector of the Xk\JM{k}Xk​ belongs to the GZ line of its tuple of eigenvalues.

Proof. We use self-adjointness to compare lines and then use separation to locate a joint eigenvector.

  1. Self-adjointness. A transposition ttt has order two, so its unitary operator satisfies ρ(t)†=ρ(t)−1=ρ(t)\rho(t)^\adj=\rho(t)^{-1}=\rho(t)ρ(t)†=ρ(t)−1=ρ(t). Each Xk\JM{k}Xk​ is a sum of transpositions and is therefore self-adjoint. Its eigenvalues are real.

  2. Orthogonality. Two different lines have different weights by Corollary 23.5. Some Xk\JM{k}Xk​ therefore acts on them by different eigenvalues. Its self-adjointness makes the two lines orthogonal.

  3. Joint eigenvectors. Write a joint eigenvector zzz with eigenvalues bkb_kbk​ as a sum of its GZ components. Since Xk\JM{k}Xk​ acts by a scalar on each component and the sum is direct, every nonzero component has the eigenvalue bkb_kbk​. This holds for every kkk. Separation leaves just one line of that weight.
