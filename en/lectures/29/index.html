Lemma 29.3 (Alternants). An antisymmetric polynomial in xxx with complex coefficients, or with coefficients that are polynomials in further variables, is divisible by ∏i<j(xi−xj)\prod_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)∏i<j​(xi​−xj​). Proof. We obtain each difference by substitution.

  1. One factor. Divide by xi−xjx_i-x_jxi​−xj​ as a monic polynomial in xix_ixi​. The remainder is the substitution xi=xjx_i=x_jxi​=xj​. The exchange of xix_ixi​ and xjx_jxj​ negates the polynomial and leaves this substitution unchanged. The remainder is therefore zero.

  2. All factors. After extracting earlier differences, set xi=xjx_i=x_jxi​=xj​ for a new pair. The earlier factors remain nonzero polynomials, so their product remains nonzero. The remaining quotient must vanish under this substitution, and the next difference divides it. An adjacent exchange negates the product of differences, since it negates one factor and permutes the others. These exchanges generate SN\SG{N}SN​, so the quotient is symmetric.

  3. The staircase. This is the Vandermonde determinant with the rows reversed, which contributes one minus sign for each pair i<ji<ji<j and gives ∏i<j(xi−xj)\prod_{i<j}(x_i-x_j)∏i<j​(xi​−xj​).
