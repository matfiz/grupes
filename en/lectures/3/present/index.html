Lecture 3

Sylow theory

By Lagrange, the order of a subgroup divides ∣G∣\abs{G}∣G∣; by Cauchy, every prime divisor of ∣G∣\abs{G}∣G∣ is the order of an element (Lecture 1). This lecture asks which divisors of ∣G∣\abs{G}∣G∣ are orders of subgroups. Every prime power dividing ∣G∣\abs{G}∣G∣ is the order of a subgroup. For each ppp, Sylow’s theorems give subgroups of the largest possible ppp-power order, show that they are conjugate, and count them. The count often forces a normal subgroup, and so decides that groups of many orders are not simple.

3.1 ppp-groups

Throughout this lecture, GGG is a finite group and ppp is a prime.

Definition 3.1 (ppp-groups and ppp-subgroups). A ppp-group is a group of order pap^apa for some integer a≥0a\ge0a≥0. A ppp-subgroup of GGG is a subgroup that is a ppp-group.

For example, the Klein group V4\KlV4​ from Example 2.11 has order 4=224=2^24=22, so it is a 222-group. Its elements and their orders were found in Lecture 2.

3.3 Conjugacy and counting

We next show that the Sylow ppp-subgroups are conjugate, and use the conjugation action to count them.

3.4 Order pqpqpq and non-simplicity

A non-trivial proper normal subgroup NNN of a finite group GGG gives two groups of smaller order, NNN and G/NG/NG/N (§2.1). We now name the groups that have no such normal subgroup.

Definition 3.13 (Simple group). A group GGG is simple if G≠1G\ne\trivG=1 and its only normal subgroups are 1\triv1 and GGG.

A group of prime order is simple, by Lagrange. Lecture 4 proves that A5\AG{5}A5​ is simple. Sylow’s theorems show that many orders admit no simple group.

Corollary 3.17. A group of order pqpqpq (p<qp<qp<q), or of order pap^apa with a≥2a\ge2a≥2, is not simple.

Proof. For pqpqpq, the subgroup QQQ of Theorem 3.14 is normal, of order neither 111 nor pqpqpq. For pap^apa, the normal-subgroup theorem (Theorem 3.3) gives a normal subgroup of order ppp, neither 111 nor pap^apa.

  • The main results: Sylow ppp-subgroups exist, are conjugate, and number np≡1(modp)n_p\equiv1\pmod pnp​≡1(modp), a divisor of the prime-to-ppp factor mmm in ∣G∣=pam\abs{G}=p^am∣G∣=pam (Theorem 3.7, Theorem 3.10); every prime power dividing ∣G∣\abs{G}∣G∣ is the order of a subgroup (Corollary 3.9), but not every divisor (Example 3.20).
