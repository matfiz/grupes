Conjugation, the class equation and counting orbits

What this lecture does

Conjugation moves a group’s own elements and subgroups. We use its orbits to obtain the class equation and to count conjugate subgroups. We then count the orbits of a finite action by its fixed points.

3.1 Conjugation and the class equation

In the regular polygon, reflecting before and after a turn reverses the turn, giving srs−1=r−1srs^{-1}=r^{-1}srs−1=r−1 (Example 1.10). The same pattern of factors makes sense for any two elements of a group. We call gxg−1gxg^{-1}gxg−1 the conjugate of xxx by ggg.

This gives a left action of GGG on itself, called conjugation. The identity fixes xxx. The inverse-of-a-product rule gives g(hxh−1)g−1=ghxh−1g−1=(gh)x(gh)−1.g(hxh^{-1})g^{-1}=ghxh^{-1}g^{-1}=(gh)x(gh)^{-1}.g(hxh−1)g−1=ghxh−1g−1=(gh)x(gh)−1. The orbit lists the elements reached this way. The stabiliser lists which elements leave xxx unchanged.

3.2 Conjugate subgroups and normalisers

The same conjugation can move a whole subgroup. For fixed g∈Gg\in Gg∈G, the map x↦gxg−1x\mapsto gxg^{-1}x↦gxg−1 from GGG to GGG preserves products, since the adjacent g−1g^{-1}g−1 and ggg cancel in the product of two images. Its inverse is x↦g−1xgx\mapsto g^{-1}xgx↦g−1xg, so it is an isomorphism.

For H≤GH\le GH≤G its image is therefore a subgroup, called a conjugate of HHH. We write gHg−1={ghg−1:h∈H}gHg^{-1}=\{ghg^{-1}:h\in H\}gHg−1={ghg−1:h∈H}. The rule H↦gHg−1H\mapsto gHg^{-1}H↦gHg−1 gives the action of GGG on its subgroups by conjugation. The identity fixes each subgroup, and the product rule for conjugation holds on each of its members. The dot in g⋅xg\cdot xg⋅x always denotes the action under discussion.

For example, conjugating ⟨(1 2)⟩\langle(1\,2)\rangle⟨(12)⟩ by (1 3)(1\,3)(13) sends the generator to (2 3)(2\,3)(23) and the identity to itself. To count the subgroups we can reach this way, we first ask which elements leave the original subgroup unchanged.

Corollary 3.5 (The normaliser index). The number of subgroups conjugate to H≤GH\le GH≤G is [G:NG(H)]\idx{G}{\Norm{G}{H}}[G:NG​(H)].

Proof. Stabilising HHH means preserving the whole subset HHH under conjugation. The conjugate subgroups form its orbit. By Theorem 2.9, the size of this orbit is the index of the stabiliser.

Summary

  • The class of ggg has size [G:CG(g)]\idx{G}{\Cent{G}{g}}[G:CG​(g)]. The class equation counts the elements of a finite group by these classes (Theorem 3.3).
  • The subgroup HHH has [G:NG(H)]\idx{G}{\Norm{G}{H}}[G:NG​(H)] conjugates (Corollary 3.5). Points in the same orbit have conjugate stabilisers.
  • When a finite group acts on a finite set, the average fixed-point count is the number of orbits (Theorem 3.8).
