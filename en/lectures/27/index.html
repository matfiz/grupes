Part II — Representations and the Symmetric Group

Young’s orthogonal form

What this lecture does

The local structure gives the size of each coefficient of an adjacent transposition on a unit GZ vector. We choose the phases of the vectors together so that the coefficients between different lines are positive. The resulting matrices give character values by taking traces.

27.1 Moves and inversions

We fix a shape λ⊢n\lambda\vdashn nλ⊢n. A chain of admissible moves from the row tableau TλT^\lambdaTλ to TTT changes the entries box by box. We count the changes of order needed to reach TTT.

Definition 27.1 (The permutation and inversions of a tableau). For T∈SYT⁡(λ)T\in\SYT(\lambda)T∈SYT(λ), let σT∈Sn\sigma_T\in\SG{n}σT​∈Sn​ send the entry of TλT^\lambdaTλ in each box to that of TTT in the same box. An inversion is a pair j<kj<kj<k with kkk in a higher row than jjj. Write inv⁡(T)\inv(T)inv(T) for the number of such pairs.

Every chain from TλT^\lambdaTλ to TTT has the same final relabelling σT\sigma_TσT​, since the two tableaux specify it box by box.

For instance, for shape (2,1)(2,1)(2,1) the row tableau and the other tableau differ by s2s_2s2​. The entry 333 ends up in a higher row than 222.

Lemma 27.3 (Moves and inversions). Let T∈SYT⁡(λ)T\in\SYT(\lambda)T∈SYT(λ). If siTs_iTsi​T is standard, then σsiT=siσT\sigma_{s_iT}=s_i\sigma_Tσsi​T​=si​σT​, and row of i+1 relative to i in Tinv⁡(siT)lowerinv⁡(T)+1higherinv⁡(T)−1\begin{array}{c|c} \text{row of }i+1\text{ relative to }i\text{ in }T&\inv(s_iT)\\\hline \text{lower}&\inv(T)+1\\ \text{higher}&\inv(T)-1 \end{array}row of i+1 relative to i in Tlowerhigher​inv(si​T)inv(T)+1inv(T)−1​​ The row tableau has no inversions. Proof. We compare the relabellings and the counts, then find a move that decreases the count.

  1. Relabelling. In each box, σT\sigma_TσT​ sends the row entry to the entry of TTT. Applying sis_isi​ to this entry gives the entry of siTs_iTsi​T. This proves the composition formula.

  2. Change of count. For each third entry jjj, its two comparisons with iii and i+1i+1i+1 exchange roles. Neither of these entries passes jjj in numerical order because iii and i+1i+1i+1 are consecutive. The only change in the total comes from the pair (i,i+1)(i,i+1)(i,i+1), which becomes an inversion when i+1i+1i+1 moves into the higher row and ceases to be one when it leaves it.

  3. A decreasing move. In TλT^\lambdaTλ the entries in each row precede those of the next. If the row indices of 1,…,n1,\dots,n1,…,n never decrease in TTT, then TTT is filled in this order and equals TλT^\lambdaTλ. Otherwise there is an iii with i+1i+1i+1 in a higher row. It cannot share a column with iii, since it would stand above a smaller entry. This makes siTs_iTsi​T standard by Lemma 26.3, and the formula gives the decrease.

 

Applying the relabelling formula along a chain shows that σT\sigma_TσT​ is the product of its swaps, with the last move on the left. Repeated decreasing moves reach the row tableau in exactly inv⁡(T)\inv(T)inv(T) steps. No shorter chain is possible because one swap changes the count by only one. 27.2 Choosing the phases

Choose an invariant inner product on Vλ\Vlam{\lambda}Vλ. Take a nonzero GZ vector on the row line, and apply the product of swaps that leads to TTT. The resulting vector can have components on many lines. We use its component on VTV_TVT​, called the seminormal vector. The inversion count controls which other components can occur.

The phase choice fixes the real orthogonal matrices and makes the unit basis unique up to one common scalar, while traces are independent of the phases.

Summary

  • An admissible swap changes the inversion count by one. The count is the shortest number of moves to the row tableau (Lemma 27.3).
  • Projecting the relabelled row vector onto the target line gives the seminormal vector. Normalising yields positive coefficients on all admissible swaps and leaves only a common phase (Theorem 27.4).
  • The generator matrices give character values by traces of products. The characteristic passes between characters and symmetric functions (Theorem 28.12).
