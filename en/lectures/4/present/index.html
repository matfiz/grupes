Lecture 4

Normal subgroups, quotients and the isomorphism theorems

We find when cosets can be multiplied. The quotient then gives a way to describe what a homomorphism identifies, and to study subgroups through their images in a smaller group.

Where we are

The left cosets gHgHgH partition GGG (the proof of Corollary 2.11). Conjugation takes HHH to another subgroup gHg−1gHg^{-1}gHg−1, and the normaliser NG(H)\Norm{G}{H}NG​(H) consists of the elements that leave HHH in place (Definition 3.4). We compare coset multiplication for two subgroups of the triangle group.

The normaliser NG(N)\Norm{G}{N}NG​(N) is a stabiliser, hence a subgroup (§2.1), so to prove normality it suffices to check that each generator of GGG lies in it. If NNN is finite, the inclusion gNg−1⊆NgNg^{-1}\subseteq NgNg−1⊆N already gives equality because conjugation is injective.

For the rotation subgroup N=⟨r⟩N=\langle r\rangleN=⟨r⟩ of D3\Dih{3}D3​, conjugation by rrr fixes its elements, and conjugation by sss interchanges the two nonidentity rotations. The coset calculation for H=⟨s⟩H=\langle s\rangleH=⟨s⟩ gives r−1sr∉Hr^{-1}sr\notin Hr−1sr∈/H, so HHH fails the normality test.

Every subgroup of an abelian group is normal because conjugation fixes each element. The centre Z(G)\Zent{G}Z(G) is normal for the same reason. A kernel is normal because conjugation preserves membership in it. Proposition 4.3 (Quotient groups). Let H≤GH\le GH≤G.

  1. The rule (xH)(yH)=xyH(xH)(yH)=xyH(xH)(yH)=xyH is well defined exactly when H⊴GH\nsub GH⊴G. In that case G/HG/HG/H is a group with identity HHH and inverse (xH)−1=x−1H(xH)^{-1}=x^{-1}H(xH)−1=x−1H. The map g↦gHg\mapsto gHg↦gH is a surjective homomorphism with kernel HHH.

  2. A subgroup HHH is normal in GGG exactly when it is a union of conjugacy classes of GGG.

  3. If [G:H]=2\idx{G}{H}=2[G:H]=2, then H⊴GH\nsub GH⊴G.

Proof. We determine when a change of representatives leaves the product coset unchanged. We then test normality using conjugacy classes and the index.

  1. Changing representatives. Suppose H⊴GH\nsub GH⊴G and choose x′=xhx'=xhx′=xh and y′=yky'=yky′=yk with h,k∈Hh,k\in Hh,k∈H. Insert yy−1yy^{-1}yy−1 to move the extra factors to the right: x′y′H=(xh)(yk)H=xy(y−1hy)kH=xyH.\begin{aligned} x'y'H&=(xh)(yk)H\\ &=xy(y^{-1}hy)kH\\ &=xyH. \end{aligned}x′y′H​=(xh)(yk)H=xy(y−1hy)kH=xyH.​ The last step uses y−1hy∈Hy^{-1}hy\in Hy−1hy∈H.   2. The group and its map. Associativity passes from products of representatives to products of cosets. The representatives eee and x−1x^{-1}x−1 give the identity and inverse. The product rule gives the homomorphism. It reaches every coset, and gH=HgH=HgH=H exactly when g∈Hg\in Hg∈H.

  3. Conjugacy classes. The condition gHg−1⊆HgHg^{-1}\subseteq HgHg−1⊆H for every ggg says precisely that HHH contains the entire class of each of its elements.

  4. Index two. The two left cosets are HHH and G∖HG\setminus HG∖H. Inversion is a bijection from left cosets to right cosets, taking xHxHxH to Hx−1Hx^{-1}Hx−1, so there are exactly two right cosets. A normal subgroup thus specifies a group whose elements record only the coset of a symmetry. The map to that group will be used throughout the lecture.

Definition 4.4 (Quotient group and quotient map). For N⊴GN\nsub GN⊴G, the group G/NG/NG/N of Proposition 4.3 is the quotient group of GGG by NNN. The quotient map is q ⁣:G→G/N,g↦gN.q\colon G\to G/N,\qquad g\mapsto gN.q:G→G/N,g↦gN.

The quotient map has kernel NNN. Together with normality of kernels this shows that the normal subgroups are exactly the kernels of homomorphisms.

4.2 Factorisation and the first two isomorphism theorems

The determinant on triangle symmetries has the same value for all rotations. Once a coset of the rotation subgroup is known, the determinant is fixed. This is the reason to ask when a homomorphism can be evaluated on a coset.

So the quotient map records the same two cases as the determinant.

§14.3 uses the same factorisation for homomorphisms from a group to the invertible linear maps of a vector space. A homomorphism from the quotient gives one from the group by composition with qqq. The factorisation theorem gives the reverse direction when NNN lies in the kernel.

Summary

  • The rule (xN)(yN)=xyN(xN)(yN)=xyN(xN)(yN)=xyN works exactly for normal subgroups (Proposition 4.3).
  • A homomorphism factors through G/NG/NG/N if NNN lies in its kernel. The quotient by the whole kernel is isomorphic to the image (Theorem 4.6). Restricting the quotient map to H≤GH\le GH≤G gives H/(H∩N)≅HN/NH/(H\cap N)\cong HN/NH/(H∩N)≅HN/N (Theorem 4.7).
  • Subgroups above NNN correspond to subgroups of G/NG/NG/N, preserving inclusion, index and normality. For normal HHH above NNN, the quotient by H/NH/NH/N gives G/HG/HG/H (Theorem 4.8 and Corollary 4.9).

Answer to the self-checks

1. Cubing in C12\Cyc{12}C12​.
