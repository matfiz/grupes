Lecture 4

Simple, solvable and nilpotent groups

Lecture 3 found orders, such as pqpqpq and 121212, at which no group is simple. This lecture proves that A5\AG{5}A5​ is simple, and shows how every finite group is built from simple groups: a composition series breaks it into simple factors, and by the Jordan–Hölder theorem the factors do not depend on the series. The groups built from abelian pieces are the solvable groups; S4\SG{4}S4​ is one, and Sn\SG{n}Sn​ for n≥5n\ge5n≥5 is not. The commutator subgroup goes on to Lecture 6, and solvability to Lecture 9.

4.2 Composition series and Jordan–Hölder

4.3 Solvable groups and the abelianisation

Abelian groups have the simplest pieces. How far is a group from abelian?

Definition 4.11 (Commutators). The commutator of a,b∈Ga,b\in Ga,b∈G is [a,b]=aba−1b−1.\gcomm{a}{b}=aba^{-1}b^{-1}.[a,b]=aba−1b−1. The commutator subgroup G′G'G′ is the subgroup generated by all commutators.1

So [a,b]=e\gcomm{a}{b}=e[a,b]=e exactly when ab=baab=baab=ba, and G′=1G'=\trivG′=1 exactly when GGG is abelian.

Proposition 4.12 (The abelianisation).

  1. A homomorphism φ\varphiφ carries commutators to commutators, φ([a,b])=[φ(a),φ(b)]\varphi(\gcomm{a}{b})=\gcomm{\varphi(a)}{\varphi(b)}φ([a,b])=[φ(a),φ(b)]; and G′⊴GG'\nsub GG′⊴G.

  2. For N⊴GN\nsub GN⊴G, the quotient G/NG/NG/N is abelian if and only if G′≤NG'\le NG′≤N.

  3. G/G′G/G'G/G′ is abelian, and every homomorphism φ\varphiφ from GGG to an abelian group factors through it: φ=φˉ∘q\varphi=\bar\varphi\circ qφ=φˉ​∘q, with q ⁣:G→G/G′q\colon G\to G/G'q:G→G/G′ the quotient map.

Proof. (i) As φ\varphiφ is a homomorphism, φ(aba−1b−1)=φ(a)φ(b)φ(a)−1φ(b)−1.\varphi(aba^{-1}b^{-1})=\varphi(a)\varphi(b)\varphi(a)^{-1}\varphi(b)^{-1}.φ(aba−1b−1)=φ(a)φ(b)φ(a)−1φ(b)−1.

(ii) Let q ⁣:G→G/Nq\colon G\to G/Nq:G→G/N be the quotient map. 4.4 Nilpotent groups and ppp-groups

An abelian group is its own centre. A nilpotent group is reached from its centre in finitely many steps.

Theorem 4.19 (Nilpotent groups).

  1. Every group of order pap^apa, ppp prime, is nilpotent.

  2. Every nilpotent group is solvable.

Proof. (i) Let ∣P∣=pa\abs{P}=p^a∣P∣=pa.
