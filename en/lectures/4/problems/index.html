    Let n≥2n\ge2n≥2 and σ∈An\sigma\in\AG nσ∈An​ have cycle type μ\muμ. Prove that its Sn\SG nSn​-class splits into two An\AG nAn​-classes exactly when all parts of μ\muμ are odd and pairwise distinct. Include parts equal to 111.   • Exercise 2 Prime-order composition factors

    Prove that a finite group is solvable if and only if all its composition factors have prime order. First prove that a finite abelian simple group has prime order. Include the trivial group.

  • Exercise 4 Simplicity of An\AG nAn​

    Prove that An\AG nAn​ is simple for all n≥5n\ge5n≥5, extending the A5\AG5A5​ theorem of Lecture 4. Follow this induction.

    1. Prove that three-cycles generate An\AG nAn​, and that a permutation commuting with all three-cycles is the identity when n≥5n\ge5n≥5.

    2. Let N⊴AnN\nsub\AG nN⊴An​ be non-trivial. Show that if NNN contains a non-identity element fixing a point, induction makes N=AnN=\AG nN=An​.

    3. For n≥7n\ge7n≥7, take g∈N∖{e}g\in N\setminus\{e\}g∈N∖{e} and a three-cycle τ\tauτ not commuting with it. Show that [g,τ]\gcomm{g}{\tau}[g,τ] moves at most six points. For n=6n=6n=6, treat the even permutations with no fixed points, of types (3,3)(3,3)(3,3) and (4,2)(4,2)(4,2), by suitable three-cycles.

    4. Deduce that for n≥5n\ge5n≥5 the derived series of Sn\SG nSn​ is Sn⊵An⊵An⊵⋯\SG n\nsup\AG n\nsup\AG n\nsup\cdotsSn​⊵An​⊵An​⊵⋯.

  • Exercise 5 Nilpotency and Sylow subgroups

    Prove that a finite group is nilpotent if and only if it is the direct product of its Sylow subgroups, one for each prime dividing its order. Include the trivial group as the empty product.

    1. For nilpotent GGG and H<GH<GH<G, use the least jjj with Zj≰HZ_j\not\le HZj​≤H to show H<NG(H)H<\Norm GHH<NG​(H).

    2. For a Sylow subgroup PPP, put K=NG(P)K=\Norm GPK=NG​(P). Show that K<GK<GK<G would contradict the first part, using Sylow conjugacy inside KKK.

    3. Show that normal Sylow subgroups commute and give the direct product. For the converse compare the upper central series of a direct product with those of its factors.
