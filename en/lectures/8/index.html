Part I — Groups and Characters

Sylow conjugacy and counting

What this lecture does

Sylow subgroups exist for every prime. We compare the subgroups for one prime by acting on cosets and by conjugating. The count often forces a normal subgroup, and we use it to study groups of small order.

8.2 Order pqpqpq and simplicity

A proper nontrivial normal subgroup NNN lets us study a group GGG through two smaller groups, NNN and G/NG/NG/N. We name the groups for which such a step is impossible.

Definition 8.3 (Simple group). A group GGG is simple if it is nontrivial and its only normal subgroups are 1\triv1 and GGG.

A cyclic group of prime order is simple, since Lagrange allows no proper nontrivial subgroup. We use the Sylow count to look for proper normal subgroups when the order has two prime factors.

Summary

  • The coset action puts every ppp-subgroup inside a Sylow subgroup. The conjugation action gives np=[G:NG(P)]n_p=\idx G{\Norm G P}np​=[G:NG​(P)], a divisor of mmm that is 111 modulo ppp. The Sylow subgroup is normal exactly when np=1n_p=1np​=1 (Theorem 8.2).
  • Groups of order pqpqpq with p<qp<qp<q have a normal subgroup of order qqq. At order 2q2q2q with qqq odd prime the group is cyclic or dihedral (Theorem 8.4 and Proposition 8.6).
  • No group of order twelve is simple (Proposition 8.7). In A4\AG{4}A4​ a class count also rules out a subgroup of order six (Example 8.9).
