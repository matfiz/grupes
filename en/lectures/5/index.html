Lecture 5

Representations and modules

What this lecture does

Block I studied groups acting on sets. From now on a group acts on a vector space by linear maps, a representation, and this lecture asks when such an action splits into irreducible pieces. Averaging over the group answers it: if the characteristic of the field does not divide ∣G∣\abs{G}∣G∣, as over C\CC, every representation is a direct sum of irreducible ones (Maschke’s theorem). Over C\CC, Schur’s lemma shows that the number of times each irreducible piece occurs does not depend on the splitting, and that the irreducible representations of an abelian group are one-dimensional. Lecture 6 turns the averaging into the theory of characters.

5.1 Representations and modules

Throughout, GGG is a finite group and F\FF a field; vector spaces are finite-dimensional.

Definition 5.1 (Representation). A representation of GGG over F\FF is a vector space VVV over F\FF with a homomorphism ρ ⁣:G→GL(V)\rho\colon G\to\GL(V)ρ:G→GL(V). We write g⋅v=ρ(g)vg\cdot v=\rho(g)vg⋅v=ρ(g)v, and say that VVV is a representation when ρ\rhoρ is understood. In a basis of VVV, ρ(g)\rho(g)ρ(g) has a matrix D(g)D(g)D(g), and D(gh)=D(g)D(h)D(gh)=D(g)D(h)D(gh)=D(g)D(h).

So a representation is a linear action: an action of GGG on the set VVV (Definition 1.7) in which each v↦g⋅vv\mapsto g\cdot vv↦g⋅v is linear. It is also called an F[G]\F[G]F[G]-module; the group algebra behind the word comes in Lecture 7.

Definition 5.3 (Intertwiners). Let VVV and WWW be representations of GGG. An intertwiner is a linear map T ⁣:V→WT\colon V\to WT:V→W with T ρV(g)=ρW(g) Tfor all g∈G.T\,\rho_V(g)=\rho_W(g)\,T\qquad\text{for all }g\in G.TρV​(g)=ρW​(g)Tfor all g∈G. Definition 5.4 (Subrepresentations). A subspace W⊆VW\subseteq VW⊆V is a subrepresentation if g⋅W⊆Wg\cdot W\subseteq Wg⋅W⊆W for every g∈Gg\in Gg∈G. The invariants are VG={v∈V:g⋅v=v for all g∈G}V^G=\{v\in V:g\cdot v=v\text{ for all }g\in G\}VG={v∈V:g⋅v=v for all g∈G}.

A direct sum of subrepresentations is a decomposition of VVV as a vector-space direct sum whose summands are all subrepresentations. This also applies to a finite number of summands.

Definition 5.5 (Irreducibility and indecomposability). A representation VVV is irreducible if V≠0V\ne0V=0 and its only subrepresentations are 000 and VVV. It is indecomposable if V≠0V\ne0V=0 and every decomposition V=W⊕W′V=W\oplus W'V=W⊕W′ into subrepresentations has W=0W=0W=0 or W′=0W'=0W′=0.

For a subrepresentation WWW, applying the inclusion to g−1g^{-1}g−1 gives g⋅W=Wg\cdot W=Wg⋅W=W. An irreducible representation is indecomposable, and a one-dimensional one is irreducible. In a basis of VVV that begins with a basis of WWW, each D(g)D(g)D(g) is block upper triangular; if V=W⊕W′V=W\oplus W'V=W⊕W′, a basis of WWW followed by one of W′W'W′ makes it block diagonal.

5.3 Schur’s lemma and multiplicities

Henceforth F=C\F=\CF=C unless another field is stated. Maschke decomposes a representation into irreducibles. We show that the number of summands isomorphic to a given irreducible does not depend on the decomposition. Schur’s lemma is the tool.

A field is algebraically closed if every non-constant polynomial over it has a root in it.

5.4 Abelian groups

Corollary 5.17 (Abelian irreducibles and eigenvalues).

  1. Every irreducible complex representation of a finite abelian group is one-dimensional.

  2. For every complex representation ρ\rhoρ of a finite group GGG and every g∈Gg\in Gg∈G, ρ(g)\rho(g)ρ(g) is diagonalisable, and its eigenvalues are o(g)o(g)o(g)-th roots of unity.

Proof. (i) Let UUU be irreducible, and h∈Gh\in Gh∈G. As hg=ghhg=ghhg=gh for all ggg, ρ(h)\rho(h)ρ(h) commutes with every ρ(g)\rho(g)ρ(g): it lies in End⁡G(U)\End_G(U)EndG​(U), so it is a scalar by Theorem 5.13(ii). Then every line in UUU is a subrepresentation, and as UUU is irreducible, dim⁡U=1\dim U=1dimU=1.

(ii) The same ρ\rhoρ is a representation of the abelian group ⟨g⟩\langle g\rangle⟨g⟩. By Maschke’s theorem and the one-dimensionality of irreducibles of an abelian group, VVV is a direct sum of lines invariant under ggg, and in a basis of these lines ρ(g)\rho(g)ρ(g) is diagonal. Each eigenvalue ccc satisfies co(g)=1c^{o(g)}=1co(g)=1, as ρ(g)o(g)=ρ(e)=11.\rho(g)^{o(g)}=\rho(e) =\Id.\qedhereρ(g)o(g)=ρ(e)=11. 

Over R\RR, (i) fails: Example 5.14 is a two-dimensional irreducible representation of C4\Cyc{4}C4​.

Summary

  • The main results: if the characteristic does not divide ∣G∣\abs{G}∣G∣, every representation is a direct sum of irreducibles (Theorem 5.11); over C\CC, by Schur’s lemma (Theorem 5.13), each irreducible UUU occurs dim⁡Hom⁡G(U,V)\dim\Hom_G(U,V)dimHomG​(U,V) times in a decomposition of VVV into irreducibles (Corollary 5.16), and finite abelian groups have one-dimensional complex irreducibles. For any finite group, each operator in a complex representation is diagonalisable with roots of unity as eigenvalues (Corollary 5.17).
