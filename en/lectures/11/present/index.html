Lecture 11

Representations and Maschke’s theorem

We describe group actions by invertible linear maps and learn to split them by averaging. Later we use the traces of these maps and the closure of solvability under extensions to prove Burnside’s theorem for groups whose orders have at most two prime divisors (Theorem 20.6).

Where we are

An action records each group element as a permutation of the set it acts on (Proposition 2.4). For permutations of nnn points, the matrix D(σ)D(\sigma)D(σ) sends the basis vector eje_jej​ to eσ(j)e_{\sigma(j)}eσ(j)​ (Definition 6.5). A subscript names a basis vector, and eee names the group identity. The preceding lecture used derived series to test solvability (Theorem 10.6).

11.1 Linear actions and invariant subspaces

The rotations and reflections in Figure 1.1 are already linear maps of the plane, and their matrices give a representation of D3\Dih{3}D3​ alongside the permutation matrices on C3\C^3C3. In Example 11.9 we will recover the orbit count of Theorem 3.8 by averaging traces.

Throughout, GGG is finite and vector spaces are finite-dimensional.

A field is a set with addition and multiplication obeying the usual rules, in which every nonzero element has a multiplicative inverse. Examples are Q\QQ, R\RR, C\CC and Fp\F_pFp​, the integers modulo a prime ppp. If aaa represents a nonzero residue, it is coprime to ppp, so Bézout’s identity (§5.1) gives ab+pc=1ab+pc=1ab+pc=1 for integers bbb and ccc. The residue of bbb is the inverse.

A proper invariant subspace lets us examine a smaller action. To split the original action into two smaller ones we also need an invariant complement. The two questions give two definitions.

Definition 11.6 (Irreducibility and indecomposability). A nonzero representation VVV is irreducible if its only subrepresentations are 000 and VVV. It is indecomposable if each direct-sum decomposition into two subrepresentations has a zero summand.

An irreducible representation is indecomposable. Every one-dimensional representation is irreducible. In particular LLL is irreducible, while the displayed splitting makes C3\C^3C3 decomposable.

11.2 Averaging an action

Start with e1e_1e1​ in the S3\SG{3}S3​ permutation space. The six permutations send it twice to each of the three basis vectors. The characteristic of F\FF is the least positive integer ppp such that p⋅1=0p\cdot1=0p⋅1=0, or zero if no such integer exists. For example, the field F2\F_2F2​ of integers modulo two has characteristic two.

The average uses the inverse of the scalar ∣G∣⋅1\abs G\cdot1∣G∣⋅1. In positive characteristic, dividing ∣G∣\abs G∣G∣ by ppp with remainder shows that this scalar is zero exactly when ppp divides ∣G∣\abs G∣G∣. In characteristic zero it is always nonzero. For S3\SG{3}S3​ the scalar 6⋅16\cdot16⋅1 vanishes in F2\F_2F2​ and F3\F_3F3​ and is nonzero in F5\F_5F5​ and C\CC.

11.3 Averaging linear maps and inner products

We use Hermitian inner products and adjoints from Linear Algebra for Physics.

We want to average a linear map T ⁣:U→VT\colon U\to VT:U→V and obtain an intertwiner. To compare its action before and after a group move, undo the move in UUU, apply TTT, and make the move in VVV.

Summary

  • A representation is a group action by invertible linear maps. Intertwiners are the fixed vectors of the action on Hom⁡(U,V)\Hom(U,V)Hom(U,V) (Definition 11.1 and Lemma 11.10).
  • When the group order is invertible in the field, the average projects onto VGV^GVG. For a permutation representation the dimension of this subspace is the number of orbits (Proposition 11.8 and Example 11.9).
  • Averaging a projection gives an invariant complement. Under the same hypothesis, this splits every representation into irreducibles (Theorem 11.11).
