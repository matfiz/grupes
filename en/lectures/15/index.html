The group algebra

What this lecture does

In Lectures 15–21 we turn group elements into an algebra, study the operators that commute with an action, and move representations between a group and its subgroups. Algebraic integers then give a divisibility theorem for the degrees (Lecture 19) and lead to Burnside’s theorem on groups of order paqbp^a q^bpaqb (Theorem 20.6). The Frobenius–Schur indicator tests whether an irreducible can be realised over the real numbers (Theorem 21.6).

Part II

In Part I we used characters to read the summands of a representation. Part II makes operators that project onto those summands. We use these operators to study representations of the symmetric groups. Theorem 22.7 builds Gelfand–Tsetlin bases by restriction. Theorem 23.4 gives the generators used in this construction. Corollary 26.8 labels the bases by tableaux, and Theorem 27.4 gives the matrices in Young’s orthogonal form. The Frobenius formula computes characters with symmetric functions (Corollary 30.9). Schur–Weyl duality relates them to characters of the general linear group (Theorem 31.11).

15.1 Algebras and modules

The operators of a representation can be added and composed. We want both operations on group elements themselves.

Definition 15.1 (Algebra). An algebra is a complex vector space with an associative bilinear multiplication and a multiplicative identity. A subalgebra is a subspace closed under multiplication and containing that identity. An algebra homomorphism is a linear map preserving multiplication and the identity.

In End⁡(V)\End(V)End(V) multiplication is composition and the identity is 11\Id11. If V≠0V\ne0V=0, the scalar subalgebra is isomorphic to C\CC by c↦c11c\mapsto c\Idc↦c11. In a direct sum of algebras, operations are componentwise, so (aj)j(bj)j=(ajbj)j(a_j)_j(b_j)_j=(a_j b_j)_j(aj​)j​(bj​)j​=(aj​bj​)j​.

Take GGG finite and vector spaces finite-dimensional over C\CC. Each group element is a formal basis vector, and two basis vectors multiply by the rule in GGG.

Definition 15.2 (Group algebra). The group algebra C[G]\grpalg{G}C[G] is the complex vector space with basis GGG and multiplication extended bilinearly from GGG. (∑g∈Gagg)(∑h∈Gbhh)=∑g,h∈Gagbh gh.\Bigl(\sum_{g\in G}a_g g\Bigr)\Bigl(\sum_{h\in G}b_h h\Bigr) =\sum_{g,h\in G}a_g b_h\,gh .(g∈G∑​ag​g)(h∈G∑​bh​h)=g,h∈G∑​ag​bh​gh.

Associativity and the identity law can be checked on basis vectors by linearity, so the multiplication is associative with identity eee. The dimension is ∣G∣|G|∣G∣. The algebra is commutative exactly when GGG is abelian, since each product of two basis vectors is their group product.

To let a sum of group elements act, we need a linear action compatible with the algebra multiplication. This is the usual action rule for representations, extended to an algebra.

A representation already specifies what an action of the group algebra must do to every basis vector. We check that extending the action to sums respects products and preserves the intertwiners.

15.3 The centre and its idempotents

We now look for elements whose operators commute with the entire action. Conjugation of group elements gives a way to find them in the group basis.

Definition 15.10 (Centre and class sums). The centre of an algebra AAA is Z(A)={z∈A:za=az for all a∈A}.\Zent{A}=\{z\in A:za=az\text{ for all }a\in A\}.Z(A)={z∈A:za=az for all a∈A}. For a conjugacy class K\clsKK of GGG, the class sum is K^=∑g∈Kg∈C[G]\clsum{\clsK}=\sum_{g\in\clsK}g\in\grpalg{G}K=∑g∈K​g∈C[G].

For example, the two three-cycles of S3\SG{3}S3​ form one class by Example 3.2. Conjugating their sum permutes its terms. The sum therefore commutes with every group element. The group centre Z(G)\Zent{G}Z(G) consists of single elements of GGG. The algebra centre also contains these invariant sums.

Summary

  • A group action extends linearly to a module over C[G]\grpalg{G}C[G]. The regular module acts by left multiplication (Proposition 15.5 and Definition 15.7).
  • The complex group algebra has one full matrix block per irreducible, of size its dimension (Theorem 15.9).
  • The class sums are a basis of the centre. The idempotent eUe_UeU​ acts as the identity on the block of UUU and kills the others (Theorem 15.11 and Proposition 15.12).
