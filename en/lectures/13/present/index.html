Lecture 13

Characters and orthonormality

We replace the matrix for each group element by its trace. We find how much these traces remember by using them to count intertwiners. For a complex representation, the resulting counts recover every irreducible summand with its multiplicity.

Where we are

Averaging a complex action projects onto its fixed vectors and the average of the traces gives the dimension of that space (Proposition 11.8). Schur’s lemma gives one dimension of intertwiners between isomorphic irreducibles and zero between nonisomorphic ones (Theorem 12.2). All representations here are finite-dimensional and complex, and GGG is finite. The bold 1\triv1 denotes the trivial representation or, in a subgroup position, the trivial subgroup. Write 11\Id11 for an identity operator and 1G1_G1G​ for the trivial character.

13.1 Traces and class functions

The trace of the action of a group element is independent of the basis.

Definition 13.1 (Character). The character of a representation (ρ,V)(\rho,V)(ρ,V) is the function χV ⁣:G→C,χV(g)=tr⁡ρ(g).\chi_V\colon G\to\C,\qquad \chi_V(g)=\tr\rho(g).χV​:G→C,χV​(g)=trρ(g). Its degree is χV(e)\chi_V(e)χV​(e). The character is irreducible if VVV is irreducible and linear (one-dimensional) if dim⁡V=1\dim V=1dimV=1.

The identity acts as 11\Id11, so the degree is dim⁡V\dim VdimV. The trivial line has character 1G1_G1G​, the constant function with value 111. A linear character is the homomorphism to C×\C^\timesC× that gives the action on its line. For example, the sign line has character sgn⁡\sgnsgn.

Proposition 13.2 (First properties). For representations VVV and WWW and elements g,h∈Gg,h\in Gg,h∈G,

  1. χV(hgh−1)=χV(g)\chi_V(hgh^{-1})=\chi_V(g)χV​(hgh−1)=χV​(g).

  2. χV(g−1)=χV(g)‾\chi_V(g^{-1})=\overline{\chi_V(g)}χV​(g−1)=χV​(g)​.

  3. If V≅WV\cong WV≅W, then χV=χW\chi_V=\chi_WχV​=χW​. Also χV⊕W=χV+χW\chi_{V\oplus W}=\chi_V+\chi_WχV⊕W​=χV​+χW​.

Proof. We use similarity for conjugation, eigenvalues for inversion, and blocks for the direct sum.

  1. Conjugates. The homomorphism law gives ρ(hgh−1)=ρ(h)ρ(g)ρ(h)−1\rho(hgh^{-1})=\rho(h)\rho(g)\rho(h)^{-1}ρ(hgh−1)=ρ(h)ρ(g)ρ(h)−1. Similar operators have the same trace.

  2. Inverses. By Corollary 12.6, the operator ρ(g)\rho(g)ρ(g) has a basis of eigenvectors with eigenvalues cic_ici​ that are roots of unity. The inverse operator acts on these vectors by ci−1=ci‾c_i^{-1}=\overline{c_i}ci−1​=ci​​. Hence χV(g−1)=∑ici−1=∑ici‾=χV(g)‾.\chi_V(g^{-1})=\sum_i c_i^{-1} =\sum_i\overline{c_i} =\overline{\chi_V(g)}.χV​(g−1)=i∑​ci−1​=i∑​ci​​=χV​(g)​.

  3. Isomorphisms and sums. Isomorphic actions differ by a change of basis as in Equation (11.1), so their traces agree. On a direct sum the action matrix is block diagonal with one block for each summand. Taking the trace adds the two traces.

13.3 Irreducible characters and the character table

Apply the trace count to irreducibles. Schur’s lemma leaves only the values zero and one, according as the source and target representations are different or the same up to isomorphism. Write Irr⁡(G)\Irr(G)Irr(G) for a set containing one representative of each isomorphism class of irreducibles.

Corollary 13.9 (Orthonormality). Let UUU and U′U'U′ lie in Irr⁡(G)\Irr(G)Irr(G).

  1. ⟨χU,χU′⟩G=δUU′\inner{\chi_U}{\chi_{U'}}_G=\delta_{UU'}⟨χU​,χU′​⟩G​=δUU′​, where the right side is 111 for U=U′U=U'U=U′ and 000 otherwise.

  2. For every representation VVV, mU(V)=⟨χU,χV⟩Gm_U(V)=\inner{\chi_U}{\chi_V}_GmU​(V)=⟨χU​,χV​⟩G​ and χV=∑U∈Irr⁡(G)mU(V)χU\chi_V=\sum_{U\in\Irr(G)}m_U(V)\chi_UχV​=∑U∈Irr(G)​mU​(V)χU​. Two representations are isomorphic exactly when their characters agree.

  3. The squared norm of χV\chi_VχV​ is ∑UmU(V)2\sum_U m_U(V)^2∑U​mU​(V)2. The representation VVV is irreducible exactly when this norm is one.

  4. The irreducible characters are linearly independent in CF⁡(G)\CF(G)CF(G). In particular there are at most as many irreducibles, up to isomorphism, as conjugacy classes.

Proof. First we use Schur’s lemma to compute the inner products. Then we expand characters using a decomposition and use the expansion to compute the norm and bound the number of irreducibles.

  1. Irreducibles. By Theorem 13.8, the inner product is the dimension of Hom⁡G(U,U′)\Hom_G(U,U')HomG​(U,U′). The dimension is δUU′\delta_{UU'}δUU′​ by Theorem 12.2.

  2. Multiplicities. The dimension counted by Theorem 13.8 is mU(V)m_U(V)mU​(V) by Corollary 12.5. Maschke’s theorem splits VVV into irreducibles, and additivity of traces gives the stated sum, with finitely many nonzero terms. Thus equal characters give equal multiplicities and therefore isomorphic representations. Isomorphic representations have equal characters by Proposition 13.2.

  3. The norm. Expand both slots using the character sum. The multiplicities are integers, so conjugation in the first slot leaves them unchanged. Orthonormality leaves just the terms where the two indices agree. ⟨χV,χV⟩G=∑U,U′mU(V)mU′(V)⟨χU,χU′⟩G=∑UmU(V)2.\inner{\chi_V}{\chi_V}_G =\sum_{U,U'}m_U(V)m_{U'}(V)\inner{\chi_U}{\chi_{U'}}_G =\sum_U m_U(V)^2 .⟨χV​,χV​⟩G​=U,U′∑​mU​(V)mU′​(V)⟨χU​,χU′​⟩G​=U∑​mU​(V)2. This is one precisely when one copy of one irreducible occurs.

  4. Independence. Pair a finite linear relation with χU′\chi_{U'}χU′​ in the first slot. By orthonormality the result is the coefficient of χU′\chi_{U'}χU′​, so every coefficient is zero. There can be at most dim⁡CF⁡(G)\dim\CF(G)dimCF(G) independent characters.

Summary

  • A character records traces. These are constant on conjugacy classes, conjugated under inversion and additive on direct sums (Proposition 13.2).
  • Pairing χU\chi_UχU​ with χV\chi_VχV​ gives the dimension of Hom⁡G(U,V)\Hom_G(U,V)HomG​(U,V) (Theorem 13.8). For irreducible UUU this gives its multiplicity in VVV.
  • Irreducible rows are orthonormal, and a representation is irreducible exactly when its character has squared norm one (Corollary 13.9). The three rows for S3\SG{3}S3​ already span all its class functions (Example 13.12).
