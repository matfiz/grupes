Lecture 10

The Gelfand–Tsetlin basis of SnS_nSn​

What this lecture does

Block III opens with the representations of Sn\SG{n}Sn​. Restriction along S1⊂⋯⊂Sn\SG{1}\subset\dots\subset\SG{n}S1​⊂⋯⊂Sn​ is multiplicity-free, so every irreducible splits canonically into lines. Choosing a non-zero vector on each line gives a Gelfand–Tsetlin basis; the individual vectors depend on these scalar choices. The Jucys–Murphy elements X1,…,Xn\JM{1},\dots,\JM{n}X1​,…,Xn​ commute and generate the algebra of elements acting diagonally in the GZ bases of all irreducibles simultaneously. Their joint eigenvalues distinguish the lines. Lectures 11 and 12 find which occur.

The subgroup Sk≤Sn\SG{k}\le\SG{n}Sk​≤Sn​ is the stabiliser of k+1,…,nk+1,\dots,nk+1,…,n, so S1⊂S2⊂⋯⊂Sn,\SG{1}\subset\SG{2}\subset\dots\subset\SG{n},S1​⊂S2​⊂⋯⊂Sn​, and C[Sk]⊆C[Sn]\grpalg{\SG{k}}\subseteq\grpalg{\SG{n}}C[Sk​]⊆C[Sn​].
